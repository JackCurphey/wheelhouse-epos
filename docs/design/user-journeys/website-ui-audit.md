Nobody without "Can edit the website" appears in any board, yet the staff banner from journey 1 offers every signed-in member of staff a "Turn it on" button. Connecting Shopify says changes will be replaced but not what happens to what is already in Shopify. And in the editor the only page heading, main area and skip link sit inside the part a screen reader cannot reach.

## High

**H1 — every editor board: the preview shows only the top of the page and cannot be scrolled or clicked.** Boards: `ws-editor-first`, `ws-editor`, `ws-editor-section`, `ws-editor-add`, `ws-editor-drag`, `ws-editor-moved`, `ws-editor-header`, `ws-editor-phone`, `ws-theme`, `ws-theme-contrast`, `ws-theme-fonts`, `ws-published`, `ws-discard`, `ws-history`. The preview is a window onto the home page that ignores the mouse and keyboard (the source marks it `inert`, which is the web's way of saying "dead"). It shows two of the six sections, "Big photo and headline" and "Shop by category", then the footer. "Featured products", "Book a repair", "Our shops" and "Words and a picture" are never visible. The "+ Add section" board outlines "Featured products" and the outline is out of sight. On the "moved" board, "Book a repair" has moved above "Featured products" and the preview is identical to before. The panel says "Click one — here or on the page — to change it", and nothing on the page can be clicked as drawn; the source comment says clicking a part selects it, which a dead box cannot do. The preview is also at 69% size, so text that is 13px on the real page appears at about 9px, and nothing lets the owner see it larger. *Why it matters:* decision 1 chose a live preview so the owner sees what customers will see; for two-thirds of the page that is not true, and an owner who needs bigger text has no way to read what they are editing. *Fix:* No choice, one pass.
- The preview scrolls (wheel, keys, a visible scrollbar) but links, buttons and boxes inside it stay dead, as the decision's "can't click through" intends.
- Clicking a part of the page selects that section: a clear layer over each section that shows an outline and its name on hover ("Featured products — click to change") and opens its settings. The list and the page do the same thing.
- Choosing a row, adding a section or moving one scrolls the preview to it and outlines it, so the owner sees the result; after a move, the preview shows the new order.
- A "Bigger" button hides the panel and shows the preview at its real size; the same button brings the panel back.

**H2 — `ws-editor-first`, `ws-page`, `ws-page-on`, `ws-published`, `ws-discard`: "Turn it on", "Publish" and "off" do not say what customers will see.** Five separate things.
- First time. `ws-editor-first` says "Not published yet" with Publish ready, and a strip: "Your website is off. Only your staff can see it — publishing gets it ready, and customers see it once it's on", with its own "Turn it on". The board does not say what "Turn it on" shows customers if nothing has been published. `ws-page` says "Last published by Jack Lewis on [date]" for a website that has never been published.
- Off, then publishing. The toast on `ws-published` says "Published — customers see it now" and is drawn with the website on. With the website off the same words are untrue, and that case is not drawn. The discard pop-up says "exactly as customers see it now" with the same problem.
- Caption. While there are unpublished changes the caption under the toolbar still says "as customers see it on a computer". Customers see the last published version, not this draft.
- Two "Turn it on" buttons that may not mean the same. Journey 1's audit M14 (taken as decision 7) describes the staff banner's "Turn it on" as publishing the whole website with one press. Here "Turn it on" and "Publish" are separate. If the banner press also publishes drafts, a half-finished draft goes public; if it does not, the two journeys disagree in words. Nothing says whether staff, with the website off, see the last published version or the draft.
- Wording left unchecked. "2 to check" on the Website page and "Starting wording · check it" on Collection and returns and Privacy do not appear at the moment of "Turn it on". A shop can switch on a website whose legal pages still say "Never" changed.

*Why it matters:* this is the moment the shop goes public. The risks are customers seeing a draft nobody finished, a website switched on that shows nothing, or legal pages nobody read. *Fix:* the first time is a real choice.
1. The first "Turn it on" also publishes the ready-made page in the same press, and says so on the button's line ("This also publishes your website as it is now"). After that, "Turn it on" and "Turn off" only change who can see the last published version, and never publish a draft; the staff banner works the same way. One press to be live.
2. "Turn it on" is greyed until the first Publish, with the reason beside it ("Publish first"). Two presses and a clear order; a first-timer who presses the wrong one is told why nothing happened.
Recommend 1. Then, whichever is chosen and with no choice: the toast reads "Published. Customers will see it once you turn the website on" when it is off; the caption reads "Your draft of Home · customers see the last published version" when there are changes; the Website page says "Not published yet" when that is true; staff see the last published version (or the ready-made page when none) with a banner that never publishes; and beside "Turn it on", a quiet line when pages still hold starting wording ("2 pages still have starting wording: Collection and returns, Privacy. Check them"), which does not block.

**H3 — `ws-history`, `ws-discard`, `ws-editor-section`: three one-press ways to lose unpublished work, with no way back.**
- "Go back to this version". The note says the old version "goes into the editor as unpublished changes". The editor behind the pop-up already has unpublished changes, and the board does not say what happens to them. If they are replaced, one press has thrown them away, and History only keeps published versions, so nothing can bring them back. The three buttons are all named "Go back to this version".
- "Discard changes". One question, "[n] changes go", with no list of which changes and no undo after. The owner cannot see what they would lose; the Website page shows only "Unpublished changes".
- "Remove big photo". The Big photo and headline panel ends with a danger button of that name. It removes the whole section: headline, the line under it and both buttons, not just the photo. No question or Undo is drawn; Undo exists only for moving a section. The switch directly above ("Show this section") is the safe version, and the two sit one tab apart.

*Why it matters:* edits are "saved as you go", so people trust the editor with real work, and a click that silently throws it away is the worst surprise it can give. *Fix:*
1. Nothing is ever lost. A discarded or replaced draft goes into History as "Unpublished draft, [date]" for [n] days with its own "Go back to this version". "Go back" while changes exist says so ("This replaces your [n] unpublished changes. They're kept in History"). Removing a section has no question and shows an Undo toast, as moving does, and the button says what it removes ("Remove this section"). One press everywhere. Costs Wheelhouse keeping drafts for a while.
2. Ask first each time. "Go back" and "Discard" list the changes ("Headline, Theme colour, Book a repair moved") and ask; "Remove" asks. Nothing is stored; more presses, and people stop reading questions that always appear.
Recommend 1. Whichever is chosen: the Website page can open the list of changes (M4), and each version in History says what it holds or can be looked at before going back (L6).

**H4 — `ws-theme-contrast`: the "hard to read" guard can be published straight past, checks one pair of colours, and offers a colour darker than it needs to be.** The board shows the owner typing the yellow `#E8C547`: the box turns red, an alert says "White text on this colour is hard to read" and offers `#7A5A10`. By hand: white on that yellow is 1.68:1, against 4.5:1 for text of this size under WCAG level AA; the same yellow as link text on the Soft sand page is 1.45:1. Four problems.
- It is only a warning. Publish stays enabled and dark. The alert disappears when the owner moves to the Sections tab, and the toolbar shows nothing. Decision 3 says Wheelhouse "says so and offers the nearest colour that works", so a warning is intended, but nothing makes it hard to miss.
- It checks one pair. The field says "Buttons and links", so the same colour is also link text on the page, and the Background box is free too with no check against the text on it (a dark background with dark text). Only white-on-button is drawn.
- The offered colour is not the nearest. `#7A5A10` reaches 6.4:1, more than needed. A colour such as `#946F0F` reaches about 4.6:1 (by hand, not tested in a tool) and stays closer to the shop's yellow. The other honest fix, keeping the yellow and putting dark text on it (`#2A2822` on `#E8C547` is 8.8:1), is not offered, and for a yellow brand it is the better one.
- The box is `role="alert"`, so typing the code letter by letter would speak a warning on each letter.

*Why it matters:* Jack wants the app as accessible as possible, and a shop owner picking a brand colour has no way to know it fails. The website is what customers use, and yellow buttons with white words are unreadable for many of them. *Fix:*
1. Keep the warning, but make it hard to miss. It stays on the Theme tab, with a chip beside Publish ("Hard to read colour · Fix") until fixed. Publish asks once if it is still there ("Publish anyway" or "Fix it"). The guard checks all three pairs (button text, link on page, text on background). Two fixes are offered side by side: "Darken to `#946F0F`" and "Keep your yellow, use dark text". The spoken warning is given when the owner leaves the box or picks a colour. One extra click, only when something fails. Adding the second fix adds to decision 3's wording and is Jack's to approve.
2. As 1, but Publish is blocked below 3:1 (the yellow at 1.68:1 would be) and only warned between 3 and 4.5. Strictest, and takes choice away only in the worst cases; it cuts against decision 3's "free choice of colours".
3. Leave as drawn. Nothing to redraw; a shop can publish unreadable buttons and nothing tells it again.
Recommend 1.

**H5 — `ws-pay-none`, `ws-pay-failed`, `ws-page`, `ws-page-on`: "Buying online" says On above a line saying customers cannot pay, and the Website page never mentions payments.**
- The two Online orders boards with no provider connected show the top switch "Buying online · On · Customers can buy from your website and collect from the shop", and a few lines below, "Not connected. Customers can't pay online, and 'Pay now' for repairs is hidden". The two lines disagree. The "not connected" message is grey (a note) on one board and amber on the other, though both stop selling. The boards do not say what a customer sees at checkout with no provider; a search of `online.mjs` for "not connected" and "can't pay" found nothing, so journey 2 appears not to draw it (not read in full).
- The Website page (`ws-page`, `ws-page-on`) has no payments line. The owner can press "Turn it on" with payments unconnected. Decision 5 keeps payments in Online orders with a link each way, and the link is one sentence ending "Open Online orders settings".
- After "Connected", the "Make a test payment" card appears on one board ("just now"). Nothing on the Website page reminds the owner if they leave without testing.
- `ws-pay-more` warns "or online payments will stop" by [date] and appears only in Settings, where nobody looks daily.

*Why it matters:* a shop could turn on a website whose customers fill a basket and cannot pay, or lose payments on a date nobody was told about. *Fix:*
1. One status line on the Website page: "Taking payments: Not connected · Connect [payment provider]" (or "Connected · test payment done"), which opens Online orders with "Paying online" already open (one press to the connect button). "Turn it on" shows a quiet line beside it when not connected ("Customers can look but not buy until you connect payments"). "Buying online" cannot read On with nothing connected: it reads "Off until you connect [payment provider]" with the button under it, and the website shows "Not taking online orders yet" instead of a basket (handover to journey 2). The "needs more details" warning also shows on the Website page and Today. Decision 5 stands: payments stay in Online orders.
2. Only the status line on the Website page; the Online orders switch is left as drawn. Less to redraw; the contradiction on the Online orders page stays.
Recommend 1.

## Medium

**M1 — all 15 editor-style boards: no page heading, no main area and no skip link a screen reader can reach.** In the rendered files, the only `<h1>` ("[Headline]" or "Collection and returns"), the only `<main id="main-content">` and the only "Skip to the main content" link belong to the preview, which is `inert`, and a browser removes inert content from what a screen reader sees. The editor itself has no heading, the panel's "Sections on this page" is an `h3` with no `h2` above it, and the region labelled "Preview of your website on a computer" reads as empty. The "Sections" and "Theme" tabs say they are tabs but do not name the panel they open (no `aria-controls`; the panel is not a tab panel). *Fix:* No choice. The editor gets its own main area and a heading "Editing Home" (can be hidden from view), a skip link to the sections list, and the panel marked as the tab panel for the chosen tab. The preview region says "Preview of Home. It can't be used; pick a part from the list" and the website's own heading and main area stay out of the editor's structure.

**M2 — drag and drop: the keyboard route exists, and has six gaps.** Every row has "Move [name] up" and "Move [name] down" buttons, so reordering never needs dragging, which is the point of a non-drag alternative. The gaps:
- Three tab stops per row (the name and two arrows): 18 for the six sections, 3 more for every section added. A keyboard user reaches row five after about 13 presses. Add Alt + Up arrow and Alt + Down arrow on the focused row name; the arrow buttons stay.
- The first movable row's "up" and the last row's "down" are drawn live. Moving the first up would put it above the fixed Header and does nothing. Draw them greyed (and still reachable, with the reason).
- After a move, focus stays on the moved row, and a polite spoken line says "Book a repair moved to position 3 of 6". The toast does that visually, but it is added last in the page, so its "Undo" is the last thing in tab order, and how long it stays is not stated. Make Undo work with Ctrl + Z (Cmd + Z on a Mac) and keep the toast at least [n] seconds or until the next action.
- The dots are a 14 x 20px drawing, and where a drag starts is not stated (the whole row would clash with "click to select"). Make the dots a 44px-wide handle with a "grab" pointer, and scroll the panel when a drag nears its top or bottom edge (a page with ten or more sections is taller than the panel).
- The arrow buttons are 36px wide (M13).
- The drag board shows one gap and a lifted card; it does not show a drag ending where it started or being let go outside the list. Say: the card returns.
*Fix:* as above. No choice.

**M3 — editing: no "saved" moment, no "could not save", and no sign of someone else editing.** Boards: every editor board, `ws-editor-section` ("Saved as you go").
- "Saved as you go. Customers see it when you publish" is a fixed line at the bottom of each panel (off-screen in the Big photo panel at this size). No moment shows "Saving…", "Saved" or "Couldn't save — check your connection". If the connection drops while the owner types a headline, they believe it is saved.
- Two people with the website switch can both open the editor. `ws-history` shows two publishers. Nothing says what the second person sees or whether they overwrite the first.
- Leaving with "‹ Website" while a save is waiting has no warning.
*Fix:* the save state is not a choice: the toolbar's "Unpublished changes" becomes "Unpublished changes · Saved just now", then "Saving…", then "Couldn't save — trying again" (spoken politely), and leaving while saving asks. Two people editing is a choice:
1. One at a time. The second person sees "Jo Taylor is editing Home" with "View only" and "Take over" (the first sees a line saying they have been replaced). Nothing is overwritten by accident; the second person waits or takes over.
2. Both edit one shared draft, changes arriving live, the last change to a box winning. Nobody waits; two people can undo each other without noticing.
Recommend 1.

Recommend 1.
