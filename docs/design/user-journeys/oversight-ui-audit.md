# Journey 20 — UI audit (desktop, Soft sand)

Audited 2 Oct 2026 by the designer helper from the 16 desktop renders of the Management oversight boards (1280 x 800: `ops-reports-home`, `ops-log`, `ops-log-all`, `ops-log-filtered`, `ops-log-empty`, `ops-today-alerts`, `ops-alert-settings`, `ops-devices`, `ops-devices-signout`, `ops-devices-signed-out`, `ops-person`, `ops-person-everywhere`, `ops-your-settings`, `ops-feedback`, `ops-feedback-shot`, `ops-feedback-sent`), against `docs/decisions/2026-10-02-management-oversight-review.md` (decisions 1-5), `generator/oversight.mjs` (`logPage`, `entry`, `filters`, `KINDS`, `alertsOpen`, `devRow`, `devicesOpen`, `signOutAsk`, `everywhereAsk`, `feedback`, `sentToast`), the pieces it changes or reuses (`settings-frame.mjs` `staffFolds` with "Signed-in devices" and "Alerts on Today"; `setup.mjs` `personDialog` with "Sign out everywhere"; `app-map.mjs` `yourSettingsDialog` with its Help block; `reports.mjs` with "Activity" hidden from staff; `opening.mjs` `today({ watch })`), the rendered HTML of the boards for the accessibility checks, and the decisions it has to agree with (Opening the shop 3, 4 and audit H3; Reports and accounts 5 and audit M10; Owner setup 4, 8-10 and audit 17; Multiple sites 1, 6, 9; Selling at the till 4, 9, 10; and, because the log names who was at a till, Signing in 3, 4 and 8, and Customer service 9). Tablet and phone are not drawn yet, so nothing here covers them.

**Not raised, on purpose.** The [bracketed] placeholders (`[time]`, `[£]`, `[n]`, `[amount]`, `[sale number]`, `[reason]`, `[Till]`, `[Second site]`, `[Phone · browser]`, `[Computer · browser]`, `[What Jo wrote]`, `[Picture of the Diary screen]`, `[date]`, `[email]`). The Soft sand look. Tablet and phone. 12-13px text that is only a label. Clipped scroll areas the renderer cuts off (the log below the fifth row, the devices list below "Phones and computers", the foot of the Alerts fold). Real example data: North Street Cycles, Bolton, Jack Lewis, Jo Taylor, Alex Morgan, Till B1, the Shimano brake pads B05S-RX at £28.00, WH-1045 Jamie Brooks. None of the five decisions is reopened: one log for owners and managers (1), three alerts above amounts the shop sets with no approvals (2), one devices list plus "Sign out everywhere" (3), "Send feedback" for everyone with the screenshot unticked to start (4), and the log opening from Reports (5). Where a finding below needs a rule the decisions do not state, it says so and offers a choice rather than changing a decision. Decision 8 of Signing in (no lock after wrong PINs) is not reopened either; H1 is about how the log words what that decision allows.

**Verdict.** The structure follows the decisions. The log is one list with filters that are labelled, 44px high and named for a screen reader. The alerts sit inside Today's existing Needs attention. The "Sign out" dialogs ask first, say what changes and give a way out. "Send feedback" starts with the picture unticked and shows it before sending. Switches are 48px with names, the dialogs are marked as dialogs and named, and the headings run `h1` (page) to `h2` to `h3` without a gap. The gaps are mostly about people, not layout. The log and the alerts name staff as fact, when at a till the name is only whoever typed their PIN, and staff are never told they are recorded or for how long. The two decisions on who sees alerts and who sees the log disagree for someone with "Can close the day". Signing someone out while they are mid-sale is not drawn. The feedback screenshot's "look first" safeguard cannot be used by someone who cannot see the picture. And the boards show only the owner's view: the manager's log, the "All shops" rows that do not say which shop they came from, and the staff member who must not see the log are not drawn.

Checked against source: `entry()` prints the person's name in bold as the second column and `TODAY_ENTRIES()` sets a `shop` badge only on the "Job changed" and "Stock adjusted" lines, so in `ops-log-all` the discount, price, void, refund, setting and website lines carry no shop; `logPage()` is wrapped in `reportsPage()`, which always passes `OWNER` (Jack Lewis, Owner), while `today({ watch: true })` uses the `MANAGER` constant in `opening.mjs` ("Jack Lewis · Manager", also in the "Who's in" list), and `officeStaff()`/`staffPage()` show "Jack Lewis · Manager" in the sidebar beneath a page that says only the Owner adds or removes people; `KINDS` has nine kinds (price, void, refund, discount, job, stock, setting, day reopened, website published) and none for a sign-out, a download or "Seen"; `filters()` offers Person, Kind, a search box and four period chips with `role="radio"` and no shop filter; `logPage({ filtered })` shows "From Today: voids by Jo Taylor, today. Show everything" and `back('All reports')` for every state; the three `today({ watch })` alert lines use `button('Seen')` and `button('Open')` with no label (three buttons each with identical names), the discount line shows its reason and the void line does not, and the price line shows "Price changed by [name]" only in the grey detail line; `alertsOpen()` draws all three switches On with a bare `£[amount]` and `[n]` and no default, one amount for "A discount or refund over", and no count of what the amounts would have caught; `devRow()` has `button('Sign out')` with no name on three rows (Till B1, a computer, Jo's phone) and its "where" for phones and computers is "signed in with email" where decision 3 says the shop; `devicesOpen()` says "Signing a till out ends whoever is checked in there; it stays a till" and "Tills are added by the owner, in Settings › Front desk › Till" in plain text with no link; `signOutAsk()` exists for a computer only, and there is no dialog for a till or for another person's phone; `everywhereAsk()` says "Their sales and jobs stay as they are" and nothing about a sale open at the till; `feedback({ shot })` draws the preview as `role="img"` named "Picture of the Diary screen" 150px high, with the warning "It shows customer names and bikes" as a plain paragraph (`msg(..., 'warn')` with no `role="status"`), and the text "You'll see it first, so you can check it doesn't show anything private"; the "Open" links in the log are `min-height: 44px` and about 35px wide (read off the screenshot), carry `aria-label` such as "Open the sale" (twice) and "Open the product" (twice), and "Show everything" in the grey banner is an inline link about one line of text high. Nothing was run in a browser, and no keyboard, screen reader or drag was tested; the boards were only seen at 1280 x 800. Contrast was not recomputed: the text and background pairs are the same Soft sand tokens the journey 18 audit worked out by hand (muted `#6E6752` on the panel `#FFFDF7` about 5.5:1; amber text `#7A5A10` on `#F7EAC2` about 5.3:1), and the "On Today" badge and the amber feedback warning use that amber pair. The failures are listed in L1 and M9.

## High

**H1 — `ops-log`, `ops-log-all`, `ops-log-filtered`, `ops-today-alerts`: the log and the alerts name a person as fact, when at a till the name is only whoever typed their PIN.**
- Signing in decision 4: anyone checks in or takes over a till by typing a PIN alone. Decision 8: there is no lock after wrong PINs, "at worst a sale is recorded under the wrong name". The log shows "Jo Taylor" in bold as the person for a discount, a void and a refund, the Person filter says "Jo Taylor", and the alert reads "Jo Taylor voided [n] sales today". Nothing says that at a till the name is the person checked in at that moment.
- Decision 3 of Signing in says the till's check-in works without the internet, so a till sale made offline reaches the log later. The "[time]" column does not say whether it is the time of the sale or the time it was sent.
- Not said: whether the log shows a till being taken over mid-day. Today's Who's in shows only a check-in time (Opening the shop 5).

*Why it matters:* a manager acting on "Jo Taylor voided 7 sales" may be speaking to the wrong person. This is the biggest fairness risk in the journey, because the people it hurts are staff whose PIN was overheard or shared, and nothing on the screen invites the doubt. *Fix:* the wording is no choice; what else to record is.
- The log's grey line under the title adds: "At a till, the name is whoever was checked in with their PIN at the time." The alert reads "[n] voids on Till B1 today while Jo Taylor was checked in", and its detail line gives the time Jo checked in. The time column is the time of the sale, with "sent at [time]" in the detail line only when a till sent it late.
1. Wording only. No new kind of line. A manager can look at Who's in and the check-in time to see who else may have been on that till.
2. Wording, and a new log line each time a till is taken over ("Alex Morgan took over Till B1 from Jo Taylor"). A manager can see a change of person right next to the sale. Costs a new kind in `KINDS` that decision 1 did not list, and one extra line at the start of every day.
Recommend 1.

**H2 — `ops-your-settings`, `ops-feedback`, `ops-log`: staff are never told their actions are recorded, who sees it, or that they can see their own.** The only place staff meet the Help block is "Something wrong or missing? … We see which screen you were on". Nothing in Your settings, at check-in or on any staff board says that discounts, voids, refunds, price changes, job changes and stock adjustments are written down with their name, and that the owner and managers can read them. The sentence that says so, "owners and managers only", is the log's subtitle, which only an owner or manager ever reads. Staff cannot see their own entries to check or dispute one.

*Why it matters:* a record kept without telling the people in it is the easiest way to lose a team's trust, and in the UK, workers are normally expected to be told when they are monitored. That last point is general knowledge, not checked against current guidance from the Information Commissioner's Office (the UK's data protection regulator); whoever advises Jack should confirm what must be said. Telling staff costs nothing at the till and is cheapest to draw now. *Fix:*
1. In Your settings, under Help, a "What Wheelhouse records about you" row: the kinds of action recorded (the nine in the Kind list), who can see them (the owner and managers), how long they are kept (H3), and "See my own activity", which opens the same list as the log, read-only and filtered to the person, with no search across other people. A one-line note the first time someone signs in: "Wheelhouse keeps a record of what you do at the till and in the workshop. See what in Your settings." One extra list for staff, drawn from the screen the log already has. It stretches decision 5 ("owners and managers only") in a narrow way: staff still never see the log, only their own lines.
2. The note and the "What Wheelhouse records about you" row only, with no list of their own entries. Less to draw; a person cannot see what is held about them without asking the owner.
3. Nothing in the app; the shop's handbook says it. Nothing to draw; a new hire is recorded before anyone has told them.
Recommend 1.

**H3 — `ops-log`, `ops-log-all`, `ops-person`, `ops-devices`, `ops-feedback-shot`: nothing says how long anything is kept.** The period chips (Today, This week, This month, Pick dates) and "Pick dates" imply there is no limit. Retention is not decided: the feature catalogue lists "LEG-05 Retention schedule" with no decision. The log holds staff names and, in lines and in its search box ("A product, job, sale or customer"), customer names; Customer service decision 9 deletes a customer's details on request but "keeps sales for tax without the person's name", and nothing says what happens to log lines that name that customer. Nothing says what happens to a person's lines when the Owner removes them. The feedback screenshot (H6) and the device history have no stated period either.

*Why it matters:* an endless record of staff and customer names is a risk to the shop (a customer who asks to be deleted still appears in the log) and an unanswered question the first time a member of staff asks. The period is not mine to invent, and it is the same question for the whole journey. *Fix:* what to state is no choice; how to set it is.
- The log page says, under the title, how far back it goes ("Kept for [n] months"), and Reports says that sales and money records are kept longer because the tax rules need them. When a customer is deleted, their name in a log line becomes "A customer who asked to be deleted", the sale stays. When a person is removed, their lines stay under their name for the stated period, so a past discount can still be traced. [n] is Jack's and Mark's to set.
1. One period set by Wheelhouse and stated on the page. Nothing for the owner to configure; a shop that wants longer cannot have it.
2. The shop chooses in Settings › Office › Your data. A shop gets the length it wants; one more setting, and shops may choose "forever", which brings the problem back.
3. Keep everything and say so. The simplest to build; the customer-deletion gap stays.
Recommend 1.

**H4 — `ops-today-alerts`, `ops-log-filtered`: Today's alerts reach someone with "Can close the day", who is not meant to see the log, and "Open" does not say what it opens.**
- Decision 2 and Opening the shop 4: Needs attention shows to owners, managers and anyone with "Can close the day" (a Staff member with that switch). Decision 5: Activity is for owners and managers only, "not staff who have 'Can see reports'", and every alert "opens the log filtered to it". Decision 2 says an alert "opens the record". A Staff member with "Can close the day" therefore sees "Jo Taylor voided [n] sales today", names a colleague, and her "Open" goes either to a log she cannot see or to a record the decisions do not agree on.
- This also cuts against Opening the shop 4 ("a colleague's short float isn't broadcast") and Reports and accounts audit M10 ("Staff with 'Can see reports' see discounts without who gave them"). The same shop would have one rule for a report and the opposite for Today.
- `ops-today-alerts` has three buttons called "Seen" and three called "Open". A person cannot tell whether "Open" opens the sale, the job, or the log (the filtered log board covers voids only).

*Why it matters:* a shop could end up with a Staff member reading about a colleague's discount or void on her screen, because she was given a closing switch. *Fix:*
1. The three alerts show only to owners and managers. Someone with "Can close the day" keeps the existing lines (float, unclosed day) and does not see these. "Open" on an alert goes to the log filtered to that alert, as decision 5 says, and a second link "Open the sale" or "Open the product" goes to the record. One rule, no names for people who should not see them.
2. The alerts also show to "Can close the day" holders, with names left out ("A discount over £[amount] on Till B1"), and "Open" goes to the record (the sale or product), not the log. They are told something happened and cannot see who.
3. As drawn. They see names and "Open" opens a log they cannot use.
Recommend 1. In every case, the buttons say what they open ("Open the sale", "See the voids", "Open the product") and the log link and the record link are not the same button.

**H5 — `ops-devices`, `ops-devices-signout`, `ops-person-everywhere`: signing someone or a till out while a sale is open is not drawn.**
- The Till B1 row says "Jo Taylor checked in · in use now" and has a "Sign out" button. The row does not say whether a sale is open. The dialog for a till does not exist (only `signOutAsk()` for a computer is drawn). "Sign Jo Taylor out everywhere?" says "Jo is checked out of Till B1 … Their sales and jobs stay as they are", and says nothing about a basket on screen, a card payment being taken, or the cash drawer.
- Not drawn: what Jo sees on the till when she is signed out (a message with who and when, or a blank sign-in).
- Not said: the till works without the internet (Signing in 3). A "Sign out" pressed in Settings may not reach an offline till until it reconnects, and the owner may think it has worked.
- Not said: because anyone can take over a till by typing a PIN (Signing in 4), signing the till out only ends the current check-in; the next person who types a PIN is in. The note says "it stays a till" but not that it can be used again straight away.
- The button's name is "Sign out", but on a till row it means "check this person out of the till". "Sign out" elsewhere on the page means ending someone's email sign-in on a phone or computer, so the same word does two jobs.

*Why it matters:* "Sign out everywhere" is made for a lost phone or someone leaving, urgent moments, and the risk is cutting off a customer mid-payment or losing a basket. *Fix:*
1. The dialog looks at the till first. With no sale open it is as drawn. With a sale open it says "Jo has a sale open: [n] items, £[£]" and offers "Sign out after this sale" and "Sign out now". "Now" parks the basket on the till so the next person can carry on, and is greyed while a card payment is being taken ("A card payment is in progress. Try again in a minute"). Jo sees "You were signed out by Jack Lewis at [time]. Enter your PIN to carry on." An offline till says "This till is offline. It will sign out when it reconnects" and the row shows "Sign-out waiting". The till row's button reads "Check out Jo Taylor". One extra question, only when a sale is open.
2. Always sign out at once, park any open basket, and say so in the dialog. One fewer choice; a card payment could be cut off.
3. Block the sign-out while a sale is open. Safest for the sale; the owner cannot act in an emergency such as a stolen phone that is also checked in at the till.
Recommend 1. Whichever is chosen, a sign-out is itself written to the log (M7), and the till dialog is drawn.

**H6 — `ops-feedback-shot`, `ops-feedback`: the "look at it first" safeguard for the screenshot does not protect everyone, and the screenshot can carry customer details the person cannot easily remove.**
- The preview is a 150px picture with the accessible name "Picture of the Diary screen". A person who cannot see it, or who needs it larger, cannot do what the sentence above it asks: "You'll see it first, so you can check it doesn't show anything private". The board shows the preview as a placeholder, so how readable the real one is at that size is not known.
- The warning, "It shows customer names and bikes. Leave it out if they don't need to be in it", is written for the Diary. Where the text comes from for other screens (the till basket, Customers, Messages, Online orders, a job with a phone number) is not shown. The warning is also a plain paragraph with no announcement, so it appears silently when the box is ticked.
- It is all or nothing: the choices are leave it in or tick it off. A bug on a screen full of customers can only be reported by sending their details or by sending no picture.
- The text box can hold customer details too, and nothing says so.
- Not said: who at Wheelhouse sees the picture and the text, how long they are kept (H3), and whether the shop's owner sees what staff send. A member of staff reporting a problem with a manager would want to know.
- Not drawn: the picture failing, or the till being offline when someone presses Send.

*Why it matters:* decision 4 deliberately made the picture opt-in so that nothing with customer details goes unseen, and the check as drawn works only for someone who can see a small picture. *Fix:*
1. Customer details are hidden in the picture before the preview appears (names, phone numbers, emails, addresses, and the text of messages), shown as grey blocks, and the preview says so: "Customer details are hidden." The preview opens larger ("View larger") with the same alternative text a screen reader gets: "Picture of the Diary screen. Customer details hidden. It shows [n] jobs and the sidebar". The warning is replaced by a plain line saying what is in it, announced when it appears. Under the box: "Only the Wheelhouse team sees this, not your shop" (if that is the rule; Jack and Mark to confirm) and "Kept for [n] months". Costs: Wheelhouse tags every place customer details appear on screen, and a missed place would be visible in the preview, which stays the check.
2. Keep the picture as drawn; make the preview larger, give it a text description and the line on who sees it. Less to build; the person still sees raw customer details and has only "leave it out".
3. No picture at all; the screen name Wheelhouse already adds is enough. Safest for customers; the team loses what it needs to find a visual bug.
Recommend 1. Whichever is chosen, the text box carries the line "Please don't type customer names or phone numbers", and an offline or failed send keeps what was typed and says "Couldn't send. Try again".

**M3 — `ops-today-alerts`, `ops-alert-settings`: the alerts have no starting amounts, and three of them can be noisy.**
- All three switches are drawn On with an empty-looking amount (`£[amount]`, `[n]`). Not said: whether a switch that is On with no amount alerts on every discount or on none.
- "A discount or refund over" is one amount for two different things. A shop that sells bikes will see a refund of a returned bike far above a typical discount, so one number is too high for one and too low for the other.
- "Voids by one person in a day" counts voids, not the share of that person's sales. A busy till has more.
- "A price changed to below what it cost" fires on a price set on purpose, such as a clearance or a sale, and once per product. A price change to a whole group of products would raise a line for each. The board's own example is the owner's price change, so the owner is alerted about the owner's own decision. It is not said what happens for a product with no cost recorded.
- Not said: after "Seen" on the voids line, whether the next void raises a new line or stays quiet for the day.
- Nothing shows what an amount would have caught.
*Why it matters:* an alert that fires every day teaches people to press "Seen" without opening it, and then the one that matters is missed. The amounts are Jack's: I have no real discounts or voids from his shop to propose figures from, and none are suggested here. *Fix:*
1. No amount until the shop sets one. A switch with no amount is off, and the box says "Set an amount to turn this on". Beside each box, a quiet line "This would have raised [n] alerts in the last 30 days", worked out from the shop's own records, so the owner tunes it with real numbers (and sees "0" or "40" and moves it). A price change to many products at once becomes one line ("[n] prices now below cost · Open"). A price below cost set by a person on purpose raises one line only the first time for that product. "Seen" on voids covers the day; a fresh line appears only for further voids after the count has grown by [n]. "A discount or refund over" becomes two boxes under one switch (one amount each). This touches decision 2's one "£[amount]"; Jack to approve the second box.
2. The same, but Wheelhouse suggests starting amounts ("Most shops start at [amount]") so the owner can tick "Use these". Quicker to turn on; the figures need a source Wheelhouse does not have yet, and a bad default is worse than none.
3. As drawn. Nothing to redraw; the first week is the noisiest.
Recommend 1.

**M4 — `ops-log`, `ops-log-filtered`, `ops-today-alerts`: "Seen" and the log do not know about each other.**
- Alert lines in the log carry an amber "On Today" badge. After "Seen" the alert goes from Today; whether the badge goes, changes or stays is not said, so it would read "On Today" about something that is no longer there.
- Opening the shop audit H3 made "Seen" leave a trace on Today ("Float short · seen by Jack Lewis"). The new alerts have no such trace on Today and none in the log, so with two managers the second does not know the first looked.
- A person can press "Seen" on an alert about themselves (the board's price change is Jack's own). The only protection is a record of who pressed it, and that record does not exist.
*Why it matters:* "Seen" is the only action in the journey, and it leaves nothing behind. The log's job is to show who did what, and this is a what. *Fix:*
1. The log line itself changes: before, "Needs attention" (amber); after, "Seen by Jack Lewis at [time]" (grey). The filtered view ("From Today") shows the same. Today shows "seen by" on the line for the rest of the day, as Tills does for a short float. One line per event, no extra lines.
2. "Seen" is also its own line in the log ("Jack Lewis marked Seen: Jo Taylor's discount"). A full trail; two lines for every alert, which makes the log longer in exactly the cases that matter.
Recommend 1. Rename the badge "Needs attention" so it reads as a state, not a place.

**M5 — `ops-today-alerts`: the alert wording tells a manager who, but not enough to judge.** "Jo Taylor voided [n] sales today" has no reason, while the discount line shows its reason; the void line's detail has "More than [n] · Till B1 · the last at [time]". The price line leads with the product, and "Price changed by [name]" is only in the 13px grey line. Nothing shows how many sales the person made that day, so seven voids on a day of five sales and seven on a day of eighty look the same. The wording is the sentence a manager reads before deciding whether to say anything. *Why it matters:* it should invite a look at the record, not a conclusion. *Fix:* no choice. Lead with what happened and where, then the person: "[n] voids on Till B1 today · Jo Taylor · of [n] sales"; give the reason on every line that has one; put "by [name]" on the price line; say "you" when the person is the one looking ("You changed the price of …").

**M6 — `ops-devices`, `ops-person`: who can sign out whom, what "Sign out" means on a till, and how the list relates to tills the Owner registers.**
- Decision 3: the list is seen by the owner and anyone with "Can change settings", and each row has "Sign out". So any person with that switch can sign out the Owner's phone and computer, and another manager's. The board draws the Owner's own computer with "Sign out" (signing out yourself). Owner setup 10 stops anyone switching off their own "Can change settings", and the list does not say who can act on the Owner.
- Only the Owner registers tills (Owner setup 10, audit 17). The list shows every till and a "Sign out", which is a different, smaller power (ending a person's check-in, see H5). The note "Tills are added by the owner, in Settings › Front desk › Till" is plain text with no link, and is shown to a person who cannot do it. Nothing says that removing a stolen or lost till is also the Owner's job and lives there.
- Decision 3 says each phone or computer shows "the device, the person, the shop, when it was last used". The board's third part is "signed in with email" on every such row, so the shop is missing.
- The three "Sign out" buttons are all named "Sign out" (M8).
- A lost phone takes at least six clicks from the sidebar (Settings, Office, Staff and roles, the "Signed-in devices" fold, "Sign out", confirm, assuming Settings opens on the first room, which I did not confirm), and the person's own "Sign out everywhere" more (Settings, Office, Staff and roles, the "People" fold, the person, the button, confirm).
*Fix:* no choice, one pass.
- Nobody can sign out the Owner's devices except the Owner, and a person's "Sign out everywhere" is not offered on the Owner's page to anyone else. The row for the Owner's phone says "Only [Owner] can sign this out" for managers.
- On a till row the button reads "Check out Jo Taylor" and the note says "Signing a till out ends whoever is checked in there; it stays a till, and anyone can check in again with their PIN". The note says "Only the owner adds or removes tills" with a link "Open Tills" for the Owner only.
- Phones and computers show the shop in the third part, where "signed in with email" is today.
- Each person with more than one device gets one button, "Sign out all of Jo's devices", in the devices list. It is the same action as "Sign out everywhere" and saves two clicks on the urgent path.

**M7 — the log does not record the oversight tools' own actions.** `KINDS` has nine kinds, none for: signing a device or person out (everywhere or one device), pressing "Seen" (M4), a "Download as spreadsheet" (which carries staff and customer names), or changing an alert amount or switch (those are "Setting changed" under Owner setup 4, but only the owner or manager who reads the log sees them). Anyone with "Can change settings" can open "Alerts on Today" and switch off the alert that watches them. *Why it matters:* the actions that exist to check on people are the ones nobody checks. *Fix:*
- Sign-outs and downloads become log lines ("Jack Lewis signed Jo Taylor out everywhere", "Jack Lewis downloaded Activity, [dates]"). The download button says what it includes ("Download as spreadsheet · includes names") and follows the current filters, so someone can export one person's week and not everything. This adds kinds that decision 1 did not list; Jack to approve.
- Who can change the alerts is a choice:
1. Only the owner can change or switch off an alert. Managers with "Can change settings" see the section read-only: "Only the owner changes these". The alerts cannot be quietly switched off by the person they are about. One tuning job for the owner; a manager cannot tune noise.
2. Anyone with "Can change settings", as drawn, and a switch-off shows in the log and on Today for the owner ("Alex Morgan switched off the voids alert"). Managers stay in control; the owner is told more often.
Recommend 1. It matches Owner setup audit 17, where owner-only actions are hidden from everyone else.

**M8 — accessible names.** In the rendered HTML:
- `ops-today-alerts`: three "Seen" buttons and three "Open" buttons with the same names. A person using the keyboard or a screen reader hears "Seen, button" three times. *Fix:* "Mark as seen: Jo Taylor's discount", "Open Jo Taylor's discount", and so on for each line.
- `ops-devices`, `ops-devices-signout`: three "Sign out" buttons share one name, and so does the sidebar's "Sign out" link and the dialog's own button. *Fix:* "Sign out Till B1", "Sign out [Computer · browser]", "Sign out Jo Taylor's [Phone · browser]".
- `ops-log`: the "Open" links are named "Open the sale" twice, "Open the product" twice, "Open the job", "Open the refund". *Fix:* include what it is: "Open Sale [sale number]", "Open Shimano brake pads B05S-RX", "Open job WH-1045".
- `ops-log`: each line is a list item whose first three parts (time, person, kind) have no labels, so a screen reader reads them as one run of words. *Fix:* the line reads "[time], Jo Taylor, Discount: £[£] off Sale [sale number], reason: [reason]".
- `ops-feedback-shot`: the preview is one picture with a one-word name (H6), and the warning appears with no announcement when the box is ticked. *Fix:* in H6.
- The four period chips are `role="radio"` in a group. Whether the arrow keys move between them, as a radio group should, was not tested.

**M9 — touch targets under 44px.** The 44px rule holds for the filter boxes, chips, switches (48px), "Seen" and "Open" buttons on Today, device "Sign out" buttons, dialog buttons, "All reports" and the feedback tick box's label (the box itself is 20px, but the whole label row is 44px). It does not hold for: the log's "Open" links (44px tall, about 35px wide, read off the screenshot), "Show everything" in the "From Today" banner (an inline link about one line of text high), and the "Open" links will be the most-used target on the page. *Fix:* give the log's "Open" links 44px width with padding, and make "Show everything" a 44px-high link or button at the end of the banner.

## Low

**L1** — the facts that matter are in the smallest text. In the log, the reason, the till and the before-and-after price ("£28.00 → £[£] · now below cost (£[£])", "status: In progress → Waiting for a part") are 13px muted text under a 15px line. For a record that exists to be read, raise the detail to 14px in the ink colour at the same weight, keep 13px for the "sent at" notes. Contrast passes on the tokens used (5.5:1), so this is about legibility, not a failure.
**L2** — fewest clicks. Reports to Activity is 2, an alert to its record is 1, "Seen" is 1, a person's filter is 2 (open, choose), feedback is 3 (name, "Send feedback", "Send") plus typing, plus 1 with a picture. Two to save: in the log, the person's name on a line is a link that filters to that person (1 click, not 2), and the filtered log's back link reads "Back to Today" instead of "All reports" when it was opened from an alert (today it returns to Reports, one more click to get back to Today).
**L3** — `ops-reports-home`: "Activity" sits alone in a row under six reports. It is a log, not a number report, and its description "everything, by person" reads as a watch on people. Name it "Activity log" and describe it "Who changed a price, voided a sale or changed a job, and when". A person who can see only part of Reports (Staff with "Can see reports") sees six, not seven, so this needs no change for them.
**L4** — `ops-feedback`: "Send" is live with an empty box. Grey it until something is typed, with the reason beside it ("Say what happened first"). Not drawn: sending offline or a failed send (H6 keeps what was typed). The sent toast says "Thanks — we've got it. Any reply comes to your email" and does not say which email, or that it is the one on the person's account.
**L5** — wording. "Kind" (the log's second filter) reads as jargon: "Type of action". "Phones and computers" row names with "· browser" are placeholders, but real names should say what a person can recognise ("Jo's iPhone, Safari"), not the product; confirm what Wheelhouse can know. "Sign out" and "Sign out everywhere" are plain. "Seen" is the existing word (Opening the shop audit H3) and stays.
**L6** — `ops-log-empty`: "Nothing matches: Alex Morgan made no refunds today." is clear. "Clear the filters" resets all four to their defaults; a button that resets only the last filter would take fewer steps back, but the page has none. No change needed; noted so it is not looked for.
