# Walk-through personas

The people every UX walk-through is walked as. A persona is a short profile of
one kind of person who uses Wheelhouse. In a walk-through you take each step
*as* that person and ask whether *they* would get stuck. The script that uses
them is `ux-walkthrough-script.md`.

**Where these come from.** Jack described the real people at his shop on
3 Oct 2026. Each line below is something Jack said, or a check that follows
from it. Anything not known says "not known". Nobody is made up: the names are
the example names the drawings already use, and the Saturday worker has no
name yet. When you learn something new about a real person, add it here with
where it came from.

**Wheelhouse is for shops across the country, not just Jack's.** Jack's shop
is a starting point. Don't design only for the people at one shop (Jack:
"i want this to not be tailored specifically for them as i want it to be
deployed across the country where theres all types of people").

## Jo Taylor — front desk and till

Based on: the front-desk staff at Jack's shop.

### Who they are
- Job: front desk and till. Knows bikes, knows selling.
- Comfort with technology: low. "not much about computers."

### What they're trying to get done
- Find something quickly and easily (a customer, a job, a product, a price)
  **while on the phone with a customer**.

### Where and how they use it
- Device: a desktop at the front desk.
- What's going on around them: often on the phone, sometimes with a customer
  at the counter too. One hand may be holding the phone.
- Time per task: seconds. The customer is waiting on the line.

### What winds them up
- Not known yet. (Ask: what's slow or confusing in Citrus Lime today?)

### Accessibility needs
- None known.

### Walk-through checks for Jo
- Can Jo find a customer, job or product from **one search box** without
  knowing which part of the app it lives in?
- Can it be done one-handed, mostly by typing, with no menus to dig through?
- Is every label in shop words (bikes and sales), not computer words?
- If Jo makes a mistake, is it obvious how to undo it?

## Alex Morgan — mechanic

Based on: the mechanics at Jack's shop.

### Who they are
- Job: mechanic in the workshop.
- Comfort with technology: not known.

### What they're trying to get done
- Not known in detail yet. From the stories: see their jobs, update them,
  add parts and work, mark them ready.

### Where and how they use it
- **Today, at Jack's shop: one shared computer for all the mechanics.**
- **Wheelhouse must not depend on that** (Jack: "i dont want the mechanic side
  of the app to be built around only having access to one computer"). Other
  shops may give each mechanic a tablet, use phones, or share one computer
  the way Jack's shop does.
- Hands: not known. (Ask: are they usually dirty or gloved when they use it?)

### What winds them up
- Not known yet. (Ask: what's annoying about the current system?)

### Accessibility needs
- None known.

### Walk-through checks for Alex
- Walk every workshop step **twice**: on a shared desktop, and on a tablet.
- **Shared computer:** can mechanics swap over quickly without signing each
  other out or working on each other's jobs by mistake? Is it always clear
  whose name the work is being recorded under?
- **Several devices at once:** if two mechanics update jobs at the same time
  on different devices, does each one see the other's changes?
- Nothing should only work on one particular machine.

## Jack Lewis — owner

Based on: the owner at Jack's shop.

### Who they are
- Job: owner. The business has more than one shop.
- Comfort with technology: not known.

### What they're trying to get done
- **Reads reports all the time.** Mostly the overview ones in Citrus Lime:
  total revenue and profit, split between the shops.
- Also uses more detailed reports with **margin breakdowns**.

### Where and how they use it
- Device: not known. (Ask: computer, phone, or both? At the shop or at home?)

### What winds them up
- Not known yet.

### Accessibility needs
- None known.

### Walk-through checks for the owner
- Is the revenue and profit overview, split by shop, the **first thing** they
  see in reports, with no clicks?
- Can they go from the overview to the margin breakdown in one step?
- Do the figures match everywhere they appear (overview, detail, each shop)?

## Saturday worker (name to pick)

- Job: not known in detail. (Ask: till, workshop, or both?)

### What they're trying to get done
- Not known in detail yet.

### Where and how they use it
- Only in the shop for a day or so a week, so may not remember how a screen
  worked last time.
- Device: not known.

### What winds them up
- Not known yet.

### Accessibility needs
- None known.

### Walk-through checks for the Saturday worker
- Does each screen make sense to someone who **hasn't used it for a week**,

## Maya Patel — customer

Based on: Jack's customers, but walked as *anyone*.

### Who they are
- **At Jack's shop today:** mostly older people.
- **Wheelhouse is for every kind of customer**, across the country. Maya is
  not one fixed age or type.
- Comfort with technology: varies. Walk her as **both** someone confident
  with phones and someone who isn't.

### What they're trying to get done
- From the stories: book a repair, approve a quote, pay, collect, buy online.

### Where and how they use it
- Phone for most things, a computer for her account (as the drawings already
  have it).

### What winds them up
- Not known yet.

### Accessibility needs
- None known at Jack's shop. Walk her with the access personas below too.

### Walk-through checks for Maya
- Could someone who rarely uses a phone get through this, with no app to
  install and no account needed?
- Could someone fast and impatient get through it in the fewest taps?
- Is the text big enough and plain enough for both?

## Access personas

Walk every story as these too, as the script already says. They're not based
on anyone at Jack's shop (none known); they're there because the app is meant
to be as accessible as possible for everyone.

- **Someone using a screen reader.**
- **Someone using only a keyboard.**
- **Someone with low vision** (large text or zoom).
