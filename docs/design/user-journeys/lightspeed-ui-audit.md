# Journey 21 — UI audit (desktop, Soft sand)

Audited 2 Oct 2026 by the designer helper from the 18 desktop renders of the Lightspeed shops boards (1280 x 800: `ls-settings-off`, `ls-connect-signin`, `ls-connect-shops`, `ls-connect-checks`, `ls-settings-on`, `ls-today`, `ls-part-search`, `ls-job-sent`, `ls-customer-pick`, `ls-job-waiting`, `ls-job-check`, `ls-today-down`, `ls-job-unpaid`, `ls-hand-over-unpaid`, `ls-job-paid`, `ls-job-fallback`, `ls-workshop-settings`, `ls-customer-ready`), against `docs/decisions/2026-10-02-lightspeed-shops-review.md` (decisions 1-9), `generator/lightspeed.mjs` (`lsSettings`, `offOpen`, `connOpen`, `CHECKS`, `checkList`, `staffList`, `startSignin`, `startShops`, `startChecks`, `strip`, `JOBS`, `jobAt`, `fallbackFooter`, `partSearch`, `customerPick`, `checkInLs`, `handOverUnpaid`, `workshopNoMoney`, `customerReady`), the pieces it changes or reuses (`shop-mode.mjs` `withLightspeedShop` and `LS_HIDDEN`; `diary.mjs` `roomsFor`, `jobVariant`, `handOverFooter`, `footNote`; `settings-frame.mjs` `roomOf`, `settingsRooms`, `lsFolds`, `workshopFolds` and its "no deposit" summary, plus `msgFolds`, `staffFolds` and `dataFolds` as they appear for a Lightspeed shop; `opening.mjs` `today({ lightspeed, lsDown })`; `app-map.mjs` `siteDesktop`; `ui.mjs` colours, `button`, `badge`), the rendered HTML of the boards for the accessibility checks, and the decisions it has to agree with (Release 1 Lightspeed readiness; R-Series sync and rate limits; Names, for work order against Job; Workshop day review 1; Collect and pay 2-5; Opening the shop 4-5; Website management 5 and 12; Management oversight, by way of its audit, for logging; and the accessibility-first and fewest-clicks principles). Tablet and phone are not drawn yet (`SIZES` in `lightspeed.mjs` is desktop only), so nothing here covers them.

**How Lightspeed facts are handled.** There is no Lightspeed test account, and the proof steps LS-01 to LS-09 are all still pending. Nothing in this audit says what Lightspeed's connection can or cannot do. Where a finding depends on that (whether Wheelhouse can see that a work order is paid, how soon it shows, whether it can read Lightspeed's staff list, whether a practice work order or customer can be removed again, whether a part on a work order is set aside, whether Lightspeed can show a part payment), it is marked "unverified" and the fix is worded so it holds whichever way the answer falls.

**Not raised, on purpose.** The [bracketed] placeholders (`[Lightspeed account name]`, `[Lightspeed shop]`, `[Lightspeed employee]`, `[Product]`, `work order [number]`, `[phone]`, `[opening hours]`, `[opens]`, `[closes]`, `[start time]`, `[time]`, `[date]`, `[n]`, `£[£]`). The Soft sand look. Tablet and phone. 12-13px text that is only a label. Lightspeed's own sign-in page, which is not drawn on purpose (it is Lightspeed's, not ours). The dashed "LOGO" box (no official logo file exists). Clipped scroll areas the renderer cuts off (the "What Wheelhouse can do" list and everything under it on `ls-settings-on`; the rows of the job page behind the pop-ups). Real example data: North Street Cycles, Bolton, Jack Lewis (Owner), Jo Taylor (Staff), Alex Morgan (Mechanic), Maya Patel with WH-1042 (Trek Domane AL 3, Standard service, £111.00), the Shimano brake pads B05S-RX at £28.00, and the Today rows (WH-1045 Jamie Brooks and the rest). None of the nine decisions is reopened: workshop only with Lightspeed doing the money (1), Wheelhouse checks the work order and shows when it is paid (2), the work order is made when the quote is approved (3), customers linked by Wheelhouse where it is sure and by staff where it is not (4), parts from Lightspeed's products (5), the workshop carries on when Lightspeed cannot be reached (6), the owner connects Lightspeed with a checklist (7), labour priced in Wheelhouse and sent as labour lines (8), and no money taken by Wheelhouse (9). Where a finding below needs a rule the decisions do not state, it says so and offers a choice. Three of the findings (H1, H2, M1) are the three choices made while drawing that Jack has not decided yet; each is flagged "Jack's choice".

**Verdict.** The structure follows the nine decisions. The sidebar has no Till, Stock, Deliveries, Stock take, Reports, Online orders or Cycle to Work. The job strip says "In Lightspeed · work order [number]", then "Waiting to be paid in Lightspeed", then "Paid in Lightspeed · [time]", in words and a badge, not colour alone. The part search shows price, stock and when Lightspeed was last looked at. "Waiting to reach Lightspeed" and "Check this in Lightspeed" are separate, and the second says it will not send again until someone has looked. The connect steps are a labelled list with the current step marked, and the checklist is announced when it appears. Every pop-up is marked as a dialog and named, buttons are 44px high, and the tokens used pass contrast. The gaps are in what the screens leave to chance and in what they claim. The two steps that need a person (choose the customer, check the work order) have no way in from the job page, and the "Check this" strip contradicts its own pop-up (H3). The "never changes money" promise on the first screen is wider than decisions 3 and 8 allow (H4). The hand-over pop-up lets a bike go unpaid and leaves no trace (H2). The Website is kept in the sidebar, but the one website board drawn still offers a Shop and a Basket that decisions 1 and 9 leave nowhere to go (H1). Around those: a Today that still shows "Who's in" with no till to check in at, a setup checklist that ticks things it may not be able to test, timing sentences nobody has measured, amounts that are Wheelhouse's rather than Lightspeed's, a customer pop-up that pre-answers its own question, and a list of states that are not drawn.

**Against the nine decisions.** 1: met, apart from Website, which the decision does not mention (H1). 2: met for the strip and the fallback tick; the fallback's behaviour when the tick is left empty is not said (H2). 3: met for the first state; later updates, cancelling and a change after approval are not drawn (M12). 4: met, with the picker pre-answered (M9). 5: met; stock wording and the Lightspeed-down version are open (M10, M12). 6: met for two states; the way into both and the Today line for a person-needed state are missing (H3). 7: met; the checklist's ticks and the staff matching have open questions (M5, M6). 8: the note "Labour is priced here, in Settings › Workshop" is on the part search only; nothing tells the owner at connect time that labour prices kept in Lightspeed go unused (L1). 9: met on `ls-workshop-settings` and `ls-customer-ready`; the account page without store credit and the "Bike ready" message wording are not drawn (M12).

## High

**H1 — Jack's choice 1. All sidebars, `ls-customer-ready`, `ls-workshop-settings`: Website is kept in a Lightspeed shop's sidebar, and the website board drawn still has a Shop and a Basket.**
- Decision 1 lists what a Lightspeed shop does not have (Till, Stock, Reports, Online orders, Cycle to Work) and does not mention Website. Website is in every Lightspeed board's sidebar under Office, shown to Owner and Manager (`ROOMS_DIARY`; not in `LS_HIDDEN`).
- A Lightspeed shop does need a website for some things: online booking (the "Online booking" fold is still on `ls-workshop-settings`), the customer's account, and the "Your bike is ready" page (`ls-customer-ready`).
- But Website management 5 keeps the selling rules ("Buying online", "Show on website", connecting payments) in Settings › Front desk › Online orders, which decision 1 removes, and decision 9 says these shops have no payment provider. The one website board drawn, `ls-customer-ready`, uses the shared website frame, which prints "Shop" and "Basket, 0 items" in the header. A customer who added a bike to that basket would have nowhere to send the order and no way to pay.
- Not drawn: the Website page, the editor, the home page, or any Shop page for a Lightspeed shop. Where a Lightspeed shop's products would come from for a Shop page is open: Wheelhouse reads Lightspeed's products to quote parts (decision 5), but nothing says they may be shown to the public.

*Why it matters:* the sidebar says "yes, you have a website" while the website says "buy something" that cannot be bought. It is also the part of a Lightspeed shop that the shop can still make its own, so removing it has a cost. *Fix:*
1. Keep Website, and take the selling parts out for a Lightspeed shop: the header drops "Shop" and "Basket", the editor's Page menu leaves out category, product, basket and checkout, and the Website page says "Selling online isn't available while Lightspeed is your till". Booking, "Our shops", the account pages, Contact us and the other ready-made pages stay. Cost: the header redrawn for these boards, and one Website board.
2. Keep Website with a browse-only Shop: products shown, no basket, each product page saying "Not taking online orders right now. Ask in the shop, or call us" (that wording already exists on journey 2's product page, `online.mjs`; I did not check whether its header keeps the Basket icon). Best for a shop that wants to show what it stocks. Cost: the "Show on website" switches need a home (Online orders is hidden), and photos and descriptions for products that live in Lightspeed have nowhere to be edited.
3. Take Website out of the Lightspeed shop's sidebar, and give booking, account and "Bike ready" pages one fixed look with the shop's name and logo. Least to draw; the shop loses any say over how those pages look.
Recommend 1.

**H2 — Jack's choice 2. `ls-hand-over-unpaid`, `ls-job-unpaid`, `ls-job-fallback`: the "Not paid in Lightspeed yet" pop-up lets a bike go unpaid, leaves no trace, and its wording is right for only one of the cases it will meet.**
- `ls-hand-over-unpaid` stops "Hand over" before Lightspeed shows payment, with "Not yet" and "Hand over anyway" (the filled dark button; "Not yet" is plain text). It asks for no reason, does not say it is recorded, and is open to everyone who can press "Hand over". After it, the job is closed as collected with nothing that says the money was not seen. Release 1's readiness note says completion and collection "do not imply payment was taken", so the job needs to say so itself.
- The pop-up says "Lightspeed hasn't shown work order [number] as paid". That is the right sentence when Wheelhouse can see payment and has looked. It is wrong in three cases that are not drawn: Lightspeed cannot be reached (`ls-today-down`), the payment check is "not working" (`ls-connect-checks`, so Wheelhouse has not looked at all), and the work order never arrived (H3).
- `ls-job-fallback` draws an unticked "Paid in Lightspeed" box beside "Hand over". Not said: what pressing Hand over does with the box empty. If it simply works, the fallback records nothing, and a bike can leave unpaid with no mark on the job.
- What "Check Lightspeed now" does when pressed (looking, found paid, still unpaid, cannot reach) is not drawn. The cost in clicks is one extra press ("Hand over", then "Hand over anyway") on an unpaid job and none on a paid one.
- "It can take up to a minute to show here" is a fixed promise (M7).

*Why it matters:* the pop-up is the journey's only guard on money, and the job gives no way to find, afterwards, the bikes that left unpaid. *Fix:*
1. Keep the pop-up. Its words follow the cause ("Lightspeed hasn't shown it as paid", "Can't reach Lightspeed to check", "Payment isn't checked for this shop. Has Maya paid?" with "Yes, she paid" in place of "Hand over anyway"). Pressing Hand over in the fallback with the box empty opens the same pop-up. "Hand over anyway" writes a line in the job's history ("Handed over before payment showed in Lightspeed · Jo Taylor · [time]") and the collected job keeps a strip "Collected · not shown as paid in Lightspeed" until payment appears. Where Wheelhouse can see payment, owners and managers get a Today line after [n] days ("Handed over, not paid in Lightspeed · Maya Patel · WH-1042"). The new history line and Today line are new kinds of record; Jack to approve them (Management oversight audit M7).
2. The same, with one more question in the pop-up before "Hand over anyway": a short reason (chips such as "Maya says she paid" and "She'll pay later"). One more press; the owner gets a reason to read.
3. No pop-up: "Hand over" works at once, the strip already says "Waiting to be paid in Lightspeed", and the history line, the collected strip and the Today line in option 1 do the catching afterwards. Fewest clicks; nothing stops it at the desk, and the customer is already out of the door when the line appears.
I did not offer "only an owner or manager can override": it would leave a customer who has paid at the till, but whose payment Wheelhouse cannot yet see, stuck at the desk, and whether payment can be seen is unverified.
Recommend 1.

**H3 — `ls-customer-pick`, `ls-job-waiting`, `ls-job-check`, `ls-job-sent`, `ls-today-down`: the two steps that need a person have no way in, the "Check this" strip contradicts its pop-up, and a bike can be marked ready with no work order to pay.**

- Today shows nothing for a job waiting on a person. Its only Lightspeed line (`ls-today-down`) appears after [n] minutes and is about Lightspeed being out of reach; and Needs attention shows to owners, managers and people with "Can close the day" only (Opening the shop 4), so Staff never see it.

*Why it matters:* decision 6 is built so that nothing is sent twice by mistake, and that only works if someone can reach the step that asks for a person. *Fix:*
1. Each state gets its own strip wording and one button, opening the pop-ups already drawn: "Which customer in Lightspeed?" with "Choose the customer"; "Not sure it arrived" with "Check this in Lightspeed" (the strip replaces "keeps trying by itself"). Needs attention on Today gets a line for jobs waiting on a person ("[n] jobs need someone to look at Lightspeed · See the jobs"), shown to the same people as the unreachable line. When a job is Ready and not in Lightspeed, the strip says "Maya can't pay at the Lightspeed till yet — the work order isn't there". "Mark ready for collection" stays on (decision 6: the workshop carries on).
2. The same, and "Mark ready for collection" asks first when the work order is not in Lightspeed ("Maya will be told to pay at the till, but the work order isn't there yet. Tell her anyway?"). One more press in a rare case; no wrong message goes out unseen.
3. As drawn.
Recommend 1; 2 is the stricter version.

**H4 — `ls-settings-off`, `ls-connect-signin`: "never ... changes money in Lightspeed" is wider than what Wheelhouse does.**
- `ls-settings-off` says "It never takes, refunds or changes money in Lightspeed." and `ls-connect-signin` says "Wheelhouse never takes, refunds or changes money in Lightspeed." — both at the point where the owner decides whether to connect.
- Decision 8 sends labour at the Wheelhouse price "so the approved quote is what Lightspeed charges", decision 3 updates the work order when "a new price" is agreed, and decision 5 puts Lightspeed's own products on it. Prices on a work order are what the till will ask the customer for. An owner can fairly read "changes money" as including that. Decision 4 also adds customers to Lightspeed, which is a change, though not to money.
- Release 1's readiness note says "No financial write endpoints are used". That is narrower: it says which parts of Lightspeed are used, not that no price is ever written. Whether Lightspeed itself counts a work order's prices as financial is unverified.
- The list "allow Wheelhouse to: read products and stock; find and add customers; make and update work orders; see when a work order is paid" is Wheelhouse's wording. What Lightspeed's own consent page asks for is not drawn and unverified.

*Why it matters:* it is the promise that sells the connection, and it will be tested the first time someone changes a price in Wheelhouse and the till changes with it. *Fix:*
1. Say exactly what it does: "Wheelhouse never takes a payment, gives a refund or closes a sale in Lightspeed. It does put the prices Maya approved on the work order, so the till shows what she agreed." Same sentence in both places and in the checklist's last line.
2. Keep the sentence and add a line under it listing what is written (work orders with prices, and customers). Less to change; the first sentence is still wider than the list.
3. As drawn.
Recommend 1.
