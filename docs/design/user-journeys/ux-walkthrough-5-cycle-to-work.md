# UX walk-through 5 — a Cycle to Work bike

Walked 2 Oct 2026 by the walk-through helper, as story 5 of `docs/decisions/2026-10-02-ux-walkthrough.md` (decision 6: walk every remaining story). This is not a UI audit; journey 6 had its own (`c2w-ui-audit.md`), and so did the till and Reports. This walk follows one Cycle to Work bike from Maya Patel's first question to the provider's payment, across three journeys, as the people living it, and looks for what breaks where one journey hands over to the next.

**The story.** Maya Patel asks North Street Cycles, Bolton for a bike through a Cycle to Work scheme. Jo Taylor (Staff) makes the order, emails the quote and holds the bike; Maya gets "Your bike is put aside" (journey 6). The certificate arrives, and Jo adds it. Maya comes in, Jo hands the bike over, and the sale goes through the till paid by "Cycle to Work · [Provider]" (journeys 6 and 11). Some weeks later the provider pays the shop. Jack Lewis (Owner) marks it paid and looks at what's owed and paid in Reports (journeys 6 and 17). Walked as Maya (phone for email and her account), Jo (desktop), Jack (desktop), and again as someone using a screen reader or only a keyboard, and as someone with low vision.

**How facts are handled.** Every finding names the screens (id and size) and the source line or decision it rests on. Line numbers are as I read them. `c2w.mjs` has no uncommitted changes. `till.mjs`, `reports.mjs`, `setup.mjs`, `opening.mjs`, `stock.mjs`, `receiving.mjs` and `customer.mjs` were being changed by other helpers while I worked, so their line numbers may have shifted. Scheme providers, bikes, prices, dates and reference numbers are bracketed placeholders (Cycle to Work decision 1). Nothing here says how a real scheme or provider works; where a finding depends on that, it says so. Where I couldn't check something, I say so.

**Not raised, on purpose.** Anything `c2w-ui-audit.md` already raised and decision 7 settled, unless the story makes it worse. Everything walk-throughs 1–4 changed. The Cycle to Work page on the website, noted for later (decision 6). The website's own Cycle to Work quote request (same). "Put aside" for customers and "Held" for staff: the audit (L4) checked this and found it consistent. Toasts with Undo that close by themselves: Your settings › Accessibility already has "Don't close things by themselves" (walk-through 1 L7). None of the recorded decisions is reopened. Where a finding touches one, it says which. Two findings say that decision 8 recorded something as "not drawn yet"; drawing it reopens nothing.

**Verdict.** Journey 6 on its own is thorough: every stage has a board, the money steps are logged, and the order knows its provider, its hold and its rule for ordering. The breaks are at the two places it hands over. **At the till (journey 11)**, "Hand over at the till" opens a sale that no board shows. Nothing is drawn for the basket, the frame number, the "Cycle to Work · [Provider]" payment, a second payment when Maya owes something, or an accessory she adds at the counter (H1). The deposit is taken and refunded "at the till" with no till board either. Meanwhile the order page subtracts a deposit that the same board says will be refunded (H2). **In Reports and the accounts (journey 17)**, the bike's money has nowhere to go. It is counted as "Gift cards, store credit and customer accounts", it has no row in the Xero choices, and the commission and any shortfall never leave the order (H3). For Maya, nothing tells her what changed when the certificate is for less than the quote (H4), or that her hold was extended or released (M3). Her order appears in neither her account nor her customer page (M5).

## The story as walked

Clicks are taps or clicks on the main path; typing is listed separately. The main path is an in-stock bike at Bolton, held from the quote, with a certificate that matches the quote.

1. **Jo makes the order.** `cw-list` (desktop) → "+ New Cycle to Work order" → `cw-new`: Customer, Scheme provider, Bike, Accessories (each a drop-down), "Hold the bike until [date]" and "Email the quote to maya@example.test now" ticked → "Make the quote" → `cw-order-held`, toast "Quote emailed to maya@example.test. Bike held until [date]." **About 10 clicks: 1 to open, about 2 per drop-down, 1 to make the quote.** How Jo finds Maya in the Customer drop-down, or adds her if she's new, isn't drawn (L4).
2. **Maya hears from the shop.** Two emails at the same moment: the quote ("gave quote [quote number] and emailed it", `c2w.mjs` line 100) and "Your bike is put aside" (line 102; `cw-email`, phone) (L3). "See your order" → `cw-customer-view` (phone): "Your bike is put aside until [date]". The quote itself says "Valid until [date]" as well, a second date with no line between them (M3). **1 tap.**
3. **The hold runs out.** On Today, Jack sees "Maya Patel · Cycle to Work quote, no certificate yet" with "Hold longer" (`cw-today`). Jo sees only the sidebar count. `cw-hold-ending` offers "Hold longer" or "Release the bike". Maya was emailed "Your hold ends on [date]", and isn't told which happened (M3). **1 click from Today, 2 from the order.**
4. **The certificate arrives.** `cw-list` → "Add the certificate" on Maya's row → `cw-certificate`: number, amount, date → "Save". "Certificate received" is emailed. **2 clicks, plus three boxes typed.** Then the order has to reach "Ready to collect": the history has "Jo Taylor marked the bike ready to collect" (line 111), but no board has the button (M1).
5. **Maya collects.** `cw-order-ready` → "Hand over" → `cw-hand-over`: the provider's checks, "Who pays what", "Hand over at the till" → (not drawn) the till sale (H1). **About 2 clicks plus one per check, then an unknown number at the till.**
6. **The provider pays.** `cw-list-owner` → "Mark paid" on the row → `cw-mark-paid`: amount, date, reference → "Mark paid". **2 clicks, plus typing.** One payment for several bikes is not drawn (M7).
7. **Jack reads Reports.** `rp-home` → "Owed by Cycle to Work providers" → `cw-owed`. **2 clicks.** It shows what's owed, not what's been paid or what the commission cost (H3).

**Totals on the main path.** Jo: about 16 clicks across the quote, the certificate and the hand-over, plus typing, with two steps that have no screen (marking ready, and the till). Maya: 1 tap, and about 3 emails before collection. Jack: about 4 clicks to mark one payment and open the report.

## High

**H1 — `cw-hand-over` (desktop, phone), `till-pay-other`, `till-serial`, `till-job` (desktop): "Hand over at the till" opens a till sale that no board shows.**
- The hand-over says "'Hand over at the till' opens the till with this sale: Cycle to Work · [Provider]" (`c2w.mjs` line 185). Decision 5 says the bike goes through the till, paid by "Cycle to Work · [provider]", using the order's provider. Audit H2 (decision 7) says "anything over £0.00 opens the till with a second payment line".
- Journey 11 has no board for any of it: I searched `till.mjs` and `journeys.mjs` (j11) for "Cycle to Work" and found only the "Other ways to pay" row. Not drawn: the basket the order loads, whether its lines are locked like a job's (`till-job`, "agreed on the job"), which frame number goes on the sale (`till-serial` asks "Which one?" and lists every frame as "Booked in [date]", with none held), the payment itself, a second payment line, the receipt Maya gets, and the order moving to Collected.
- An accessory added at the counter has no way in. The provider's setting says whether accessories are "Inside what the provider pays" or "The customer pays for them at collection" (`cw-settings-provider`, line 206), but the hand-over has no "add something", and the only change is More › "Change the accessories", which makes a revised quote (`cw-more`).
- The till's own row says "Cycle to Work scheme — [Scheme name] — paid by the scheme against a certificate" (`till.mjs`, about line 219, `till-pay-other`). Nothing says what happens if Jo picks it for a basket that didn't come from an order.

*Why it matters:* this is where Maya gets her bike and the shop records the sale that the provider will pay for. Jo presses "Hand over at the till" and nothing drawn says what she'll see. If Maya adds a lock, or her certificate left £[£] for her to pay, nothing shows how it's taken. The frame number that goes on Maya's bike record and warranty isn't tied to the frame that was held. *Fix, no choice (touches Selling at the till 15, Cycle to Work 5 and 7 (audit H1, H2)):* draw the Cycle to Work sale at the till, modelled on `till-job`.
- `till-c2w`: the basket opens as "Maya Patel · Cycle to Work · quote [quote number]". The bike and the order's accessories are locked lines, marked "on the order". The bike line shows the held frame number, already picked. "Take payment" opens with "Cycle to Work · [Provider] · £[£]" as the first, one-tap method.
- `till-c2w-extra`: Maya adds [Accessory] at the counter. It goes on as an ordinary line under the order's lines. Payment becomes two lines, "Cycle to Work · [Provider] £[£]" and "Maya pays £[£]", with Card first for her part. The second line is the same one audit H2 asks for when the certificate leaves her something to pay.
- `till-c2w-paid`: the receipt shows both payments, and the bike is marked collected with the frame number. The order moves to Collected and its history gets the sale number.
- The "Other ways to pay" row reads "Cycle to Work — for a bike on a Cycle to Work order". Picked over a basket with no order, it lists the orders that are Ready to collect. With none, it says "Start from Front desk › Cycle to Work".

**H2 — `cw-order-deposit-paid`, `cw-order-deposit`, `cw-settings-deposit` (desktop), `till-deposit`, `till-refund`: the deposit is subtracted from what Maya pays at collection on the same board that says it will be refunded, and neither taking it nor refunding it is drawn at the till.**
- `cw-order-deposit-paid` says "When the certificate arrives, Wheelhouse puts the deposit refund on the till for the next person to give back — your rule in Settings" (line 130). Its "Who pays what" shows "Deposit Maya has paid −£[£]" and "Maya pays at collection £[£]" (line 121, rendered). The settings drawn have "Refund the deposit" chosen (line 201), and the quote says "refunded when your certificate reaches us" (line 153).
- So on the drawn rule, the deposit is both given back and taken off the price. Only "Count it toward the price" should take it off.
- "Take the deposit at the till" (line 129) leads to no board. The till's "Take a deposit" is for part-paying a sale ("Part now, the rest later — kept with the customer and the sale", `till-deposit`), not an order with no sale yet. "On the till for the next person to give back" isn't drawn either. It doesn't say who gives it back, on which till, or how Maya gets it if she isn't in the shop.

*Why it matters:* a promise about money breaks either way. Either Maya gets her deposit back and is also charged less at collection, so the shop loses the deposit, or she's told it's refunded and then it isn't. The refund depends on "the next person" at a till noticing something no board shows. *Fix (touches Cycle to Work 4 and 7 (audit H2, M8); neither reopened):*
1. "Who pays what" follows the shop's deposit rule. With "Refund the deposit", the line reads "Deposit £[£] · refunded when the certificate arrives" and isn't subtracted. With "Count it toward the price", it's subtracted, as drawn. "Take the deposit at the till" opens the till with one line, "Deposit · Maya Patel's Cycle to Work order", that is paid and linked back to the order (`till-c2w-deposit`). With the refund rule, adding the certificate puts "Refund the £[£] deposit" as the order's Next step, for whoever adds it. It opens the till's refund of that deposit sale, the way `till-refund` already does for a sale, back the way it was paid. Until it's done, a line on the list and on Today reads "Deposit to refund · Maya Patel". The "Certificate received" email tells Maya "Your £[£] deposit is being refunded".
2. Keep the deposit until collection and give it back there as a line on the hand-over sale ("Deposit back −£[£]"), whatever the rule. One fewer visit, but it changes what "Refunded when the certificate arrives" means, and the quote's sentence with it.
