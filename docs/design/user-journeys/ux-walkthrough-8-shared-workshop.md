# UX walk-through 8 — the workshop on a shared computer, and several mechanics at once

Walked 3 Oct 2026 by the walk-through helper, following `ux-walkthrough-script.md`. This story is not one of the seven in the script's table. It tests one requirement Jack gave on 3 Oct 2026: at his shop all the mechanics share **one** computer today, but "i dont want the mechanic side of the app to be built around only having access to one computer" (`personas.md` lines 68–71). So the workshop has to work (a) for several mechanics sharing one desktop, (b) for mechanics each on their own tablet or phone, at the same time, and (c) for a mix of the two.

**The story.** Thursday 17 September at North Street Cycles, Bolton (the drawings' example week). Jo Taylor books Maya Patel's Trek Domane AL 3 in at the front desk (WH-1042, Standard service, Hook 3). Alex Morgan (Mechanic) works it from the Workshop diary, adds the Shimano brake pads B05S-RX, ticks the work and the checklist and presses "Mark ready for collection" (£111.00 approved). Jo also works in the workshop (Staff, "works in the workshop" — `setup.mjs` line 208), so she is the drawings' second mechanic: she has her own diary column, "Jo Taylor", with Oliver Chen's Brompton C Line, WH-1068, at 13:30 that day (`diary.mjs` line 398). No third mechanic is drawn; where the story needs one it says "[second mechanic]". On Saturday a Saturday worker (no name yet) is on the till and front desk. That is my assumption: `personas.md` says the Saturday worker's job is "not known in detail" (till, workshop or both). Walked twice for every workshop step, as `personas.md` asks: on one shared desktop, and on a tablet each. Also walked as someone using a screen reader, someone using only a keyboard, and someone with low vision.

**How facts are handled.** Every finding names the screens (id and size) and the line it rests on. "Drawings" means the generator in `docs/design/user-journeys/generator/` and the boards it renders. "Built app" means code under `server/` and `src/`, and is kept separate. Where I searched for something and did not find it, I say what I searched; where I could not check something, I say "not checked".

**Not raised, on purpose.** Anything a journey's own UI audit already covers, unless this story makes it worse. The Soft sand look. No recorded decision is reopened; where a finding touches one, it says which, by file and number.

**Verdict.** The drawings are built around **one person per signed-in device**. That works well on a personal tablet: Alex signs in once, lands on his own diary ("Me"), and everything he does carries his name. It does not work on a shared desktop. The only way to change who is using the staff app is to sign out and sign in again with an email and password. Nothing on the job pop-up says whose name the work is going under. A computer left signed in as the owner gives a mechanic Reports, Settings and a way to see a new till PIN for the owner. The till already solves the shared-computer problem — it stays signed in, and people take over by typing their four-digit PIN — but nothing in the workshop uses that pattern. Several devices at once is not drawn anywhere in the workshop. The only drawn examples of people working together are stock take ("Staff join on their phones") and the website editor ("One person edits at a time").

## The story as walked

Clicks are taps or clicks on the main path; typing is listed separately. Sign-in uses WorkOS's own pages, which are drawn only roughly (`workos-signin`: "Email", "Continue", "Approximate look"). The password step is on WorkOS's page and is not drawn, so it is counted from the sign-in spec (staff sign in "with email and password", `app-map.mjs` line 350).

**A. On one shared desktop (Jack's shop today)**

1. **Alex signs in for the day** — `workos-signin` (desktop): email, Continue, then password. **2 clicks, 2 fields.** He lands on Workshop › Diary (App map decision 11), opened on "Me" (`diary-mechanic`, desktop). The sidebar's foot reads "AM · Alex Morgan · Mechanic · Sign out". The sidebar shows only Diary and Overview (`diary.mjs` lines 114–117: the Mechanic role, K, sees only the Workshop room).
2. **Jo books Maya's bike in at the front desk** — on her own desktop, signed in as Jo: Today's "Book in" → `job-overview` → "Book in" → `job-book-in` (desktop), tag printed. **2 clicks.** The hand-over to the workshop is the diary block and the printed tag. Nothing is sent to a mechanic. Alex sees it because his diary shows the job.
3. **Alex opens WH-1042 and starts work** — tap the 11:30 block, then "Start work" (`job-book-in`). **2 clicks.** The job opens as a pop-up that covers almost the whole screen (Workshop day 16). It covers the sidebar name too (checked by screenshot, `job-mechanic`, desktop: only "Mechanic" and part of "Sign out" show, dimmed, at the bottom left).
4. **Alex adds the pads and does the work** — "Scan barcode", scan B05S-RX, tick three Done boxes, open "Full service checklist", tick items, "Done", "Mark ready for collection" (`job-mechanic`, `job-checklist`). **About 20 clicks** (walk-through 1 counted about 26 with the quote). The strip then reads "Alex marked it ready at 15:30. “Bike ready” goes to Maya by text in 1 minute." with Undo (`job-finished`, `diary.mjs` line 2295).
5. **Alex goes to lunch without signing out. Jo comes to the same computer** to work on WH-1068 in her column at 13:30. To work under her own name: "Sign out" → `auth-signedout` "You’ve signed out · Close this window, or sign in again." → "Sign in again" → email, Continue → password → Staff lands on "Front desk › Till (or the page they last used)" (App map decision 11) → Diary → WH-1068. **5–6 clicks, 2 fields**, before she touches the job. Or she just taps WH-1068 on Alex's open diary: **1 click**, and everything she does is recorded as Alex (H1).
6. **Alex comes back.** The same again: Sign out, Sign in again, email, password. **4 clicks, 2 fields.**
7. **Several swaps a day.** With the two of them swapping four times, that is about 20 clicks and 8 typed fields (an email and a password each time) that do no work. A till takeover is four digits (Signing in decision 4).

**B. A tablet each**

1. **Alex on his tablet, Jo on hers** — each signs in once (built app: a session lasts 30 days, `server/auth.js` line 17). Alex lands on "Me" (`diary-mechanic`, tablet). On a tablet the name shows only as "AM" with a cog at the foot of the rail (screenshot, `staff-app-mechanic`, tablet). **0 clicks** to swap: there is nothing to swap.
2. **Alex works WH-1042 as in A3–A4.** Same clicks.
3. **At the same time Jo, at the front desk, takes Maya's phone call** and opens WH-1042 to add "customer called" to the notes. What Alex sees, and what happens if they both type in the one notes box (Workshop day 38) or both change a line: **not drawn** (H2).
4. **The shared-queue job WH-1046** ("Shared queue · 60 min", `diary.mjs` line 491) sits in the No time row on both tablets. Both could start it. **Not drawn** (M4).

**C. Saturday**

1. **The Saturday worker on the till** — `till-checkin` (desktop): types a PIN. **4 digits.** "Your PIN checks you in and puts your name on sales. It works even when the internet is down."
2. **A bike to hand over, paid online** — the "Hand over" button is on the job page (`cp-ready-paid`), in the staff app. From the till: rest on the rail, then Diary, then the job, then Hand over. **About 3–4 clicks**, for someone who has their own email sign-in. A till-only worker (`set-staff-invite-till-only`: "They don’t sign in to Wheelhouse, so they can’t open anything away from the till") has no such path (M6). On the till the sidebar's foot reads "Jo Taylor · Staff · Sign out" (`till-rail-open`). The till itself was set up "Signed in as Jack Lewis (Owner)" (`till-setup`). Whose role and name the rest of the shop uses on a till is not said for someone with an email sign-in, and a till-only worker cannot open the job page at all (M6).

**Totals.** On a shared desktop, each swap between mechanics costs 4–6 clicks and an email and password, against 4 digits at the till. On a tablet each, swapping costs nothing, but nothing is drawn for two people on one job.

## High

**H1 — `diary-mechanic`, `job-mechanic`, `job-finished`, `job-checklist` (desktop); `auth-signedout`, `workos-signin` (desktop); compare `till-checkin`, `till-rail-open` (desktop): on a shared computer there is no quick way to change who is working, so work is recorded under whoever signed in.**
- The staff app is "Signed in with email and password" (`map`, `app-map.mjs` line 350). The only identity control on any workshop page is the sidebar's foot: name, role and "Sign out" (`diary.mjs` `shellDesktop`, line 169). Signing out leads to `auth-signedout` ("You’ve signed out · Close this window, or sign in again.") and then WorkOS's page. That is 4–6 clicks plus an email and password for every swap (story steps A5–A6).
- Searched the generator (`*.mjs`) for "switch user", "switch person", "who is using", "shared computer", "Not you", "My jobs" and "Who’s working". The only "Not you" and "Who’s working today?" are in `stage1.mjs` ("Who’s working today?" at line 256, "Not you? Go back" at lines 263–264), the first drawings of the till, which `signin.mjs` replaced. The other "Not you?" is on a customer page (`book.mjs` line 68). Nothing in the workshop.
- The till already does what a shared workshop computer needs. It "stays signed in, works offline, and staff check in with their PIN" (`till-setup`; `map`: "ON A REGISTERED TILL"). Anyone takes over by typing a PIN alone (Signing in decision 4). The till bar's "Serving: Jo Taylor" switches who is serving (App map decision 13). Everyone who uses the till has a unique PIN (Signing in decisions 4, 6–7). That is not every mechanic: a mechanic uses the till only with the "Can use the till" switch on (Owner setup 9), and a person with no PIN sees "No PIN yet · Get your PIN" (`your-settings`).
- What goes under the signed-in name: the ready strip "Alex marked it ready at 15:30" (`diary.mjs` line 2295); "Created Thu 17 Sep · by Jo Taylor"; the activity log's "Job changed" lines (Management oversight decision 1); "What Wheelhouse records about you … price and job changes are kept with your name" (`your-settings`); and the planned mechanic sign-off, which puts a mechanic's name to a job "so if a job comes back the shop can see who worked on it" (Workshop day 64, not yet designed).

*Why it matters:* at Jack's shop today the workshop shares one computer. Nobody will type an email and password four times a day. So the computer stays signed in as one person, and every part, tick, note and "marked it ready" goes under that name. That breaks the activity log, the sign-off (Workshop day 64) and any "who did this job" question when a bike comes back. It is the walk-through check in `personas.md`: "can mechanics swap over quickly without signing each other out or working on each other's jobs by mistake?" On the drawings, no. *Fix:*
1. **A "workshop computer", set up like a till.** The owner sets the computer up once ("Make this computer a workshop computer", beside "Make this computer a till"). It stays signed in as the shop. Each person takes over by typing their till PIN: one step, the PIN they already have. A bar along the top of every workshop page reads "Working: Alex Morgan · Switch", like the till's "Serving: Jo Taylor". Everything done on it is recorded under that name, and the menu shows that person's role. Signed-in devices lists it ("Workshop computer · Alex Morgan working"). Best for Jack's shop today: four digits per swap, and nothing new to learn. Costs one set-up screen, the top bar, and one row in Signed-in devices. A four-digit PIN is a check of who is there, not strong security (offline spec, `2026-09-27-release-2-foundations-offline-design.md` line 83). Signing in decision 8 already judged that good enough for a till. The same spec goes on (lines 83–86) that the PIN "is not a sign-in for anything beyond the till", so using it to take over a workshop computer would widen what the PIN is for, and Jack would be choosing that. It would also need every workshop user to have a PIN, which "Can use the till" does not give a mechanic by default.
2. **Keep email sign-in, and add "Switch person"**, which keeps the computer signed in and asks only for the next person's email and password. No new kind of computer. Still an email and password at every swap, so in practice it would not be used.
3. **Tap your name, then type your PIN.** Helps people who forget whether they switched. One more tap each time, and Signing in decision 4 chose PIN alone over name-then-PIN for the till, so the till and the workshop would work differently.

Recommend 1. *Touches Signing in 4, 6, 7, 8; App map 11 and 13; Management oversight 1, 3 and 6 (its audit H1: "At a till, the name is whoever’s PIN was last typed" would then apply to the workshop computer too); Workshop day 64; the offline spec's "not a sign-in for anything beyond the till" (line 86).*

**H2 — `diary`, `diary-mechanic`, `job-mechanic`, `job-quote`, `dq-job-quote` (desktop, tablet): several devices at once is not drawn anywhere in the workshop.**
- Searched `diary.mjs`, `job-page.mjs`, `quote.mjs`, `collect.mjs`, `opening.mjs` and `stock.mjs` for "live", "real time", "someone else", "another device", "same time", "is editing", "changed by" and "Updated by". Nothing in the diary or on the job page. `job-page.mjs` mentions "conflict" only about layout (line 249).
- The two places the drawings *do* draw several people: stock take, "Count the whole shop, a category or an area. Staff join on their phones; the shop stays open", with "Jo Taylor and Alex Morgan counting" on the count (`stock.mjs` lines 264–265: the "Staff join on their phones" line is on `tk-hub`, the manager's board; "Jo Taylor and Alex Morgan counting" and "Join" are on both it and `tk-hub-staff`); and the website editor, "Jack Lewis is editing Home · One person edits at a time, so nobody’s work is overwritten" with "View only" and "Take over" (`ws-editor-taken`, `website.mjs` line 355).
- The job page's notes are "one large text box" with no names or times on what staff write (Workshop day 38), and writing notes "is the main thing" staff do on a job (Workshop day 31). Jo on the phone with Maya and Alex at the bike are the two people most likely to type in it at the same moment.
- Built app (not drawings): every job change carries the version the screen last read. If the job changed in the meantime the server refuses it with "This job changed while you were looking at it. Reload and try again." (`server/server.js` line 3382), and the job pop-up shows "This job changed while you were looking at it." and reloads the job (`src/screens/diary/job-dialog.tsx` lines 96–97 for status actions, 143 and 163 for parts). For notes it adds "Your words are still in the box." (lines 123–124), so a typed note is not lost. The diary's Waiting for you column refreshes every 60 seconds (`src/screens/diary/diary-page.tsx` line 349). The week's jobs have no timed refresh set (lines 350–356); the staff app's query settings (`src/staff/app-shell.tsx` lines 65–73) do not switch off the library's default refetch on returning to the tab, which I did not run. So the built app already protects against overwriting, in a way the drawings don't show.

*Why it matters:* this is half of Jack's requirement: "possibly at the same time". With a tablet each, Alex can mark WH-1042 ready while Jo is typing a note into it, or two people can start the same job, and nothing drawn says what each of them sees. Nothing drawn says what happens to notes typed into the one box (the built app keeps the second person's words in the box and asks them to save again). *Fix:*
1. **Live diary and job, with a "who else is here" line.** A change on any device shows on every other device within a few seconds. A job open somewhere else says "Jo Taylor has this job open". The notes box saves as people type and shows the other person's words arriving. If two people change the same line at once, the second sees "Alex changed this a moment ago" with his version and "Keep mine" or "Keep Alex’s". Best for real several-at-once work, and nothing is lost. The most work to build; the shared notes box is the hardest part.
2. **One person changes a job at a time, like the website editor.** The diary still updates live. A second person opening the job sees "Alex Morgan is working on this job" with "View only" and "Take over". Simple, and nothing is overwritten. Jo, on the phone, has to take the job over from Alex to add one line.
3. **Draw what the built app already does.** On saving, "This job changed while you were looking at it", then reload. No new design. In the built app the second person's notes stay in the box and they save again; the diary does not update by itself, apart from the 60-second Waiting column.

Recommend 1. If 1 is too big for Release 2, use 2 for the job and keep the diary live. *Touches Workshop day 16, 31, 38.*

**H3 — `staff-app`, `your-settings`, `pin-change` (desktop); `ops-devices`; `auth-expired`: a shared computer left signed in as the owner or a manager opens money, settings and the owner's till PIN, and nothing says when it signs itself out.**
- Owners and Managers see Reports, Website and Settings (roles "OM", `diary.mjs` line 117). Settings › Office holds Staff and roles and Signed-in devices (Management oversight 3). Reports shows takings and, with "Can see costs and margin", margins (Owner setup later change, 1 Oct).
- Your settings has "Till PIN · Change PIN" (`your-settings`; Signing in decision 6). "Change PIN" shows a new PIN on screen: "Your new till PIN · Wheelhouse picked this for you — nobody else at the shop has it. Learn it before you close this.", then "Keep this PIN" (`pin-change`, `signin.mjs` line 155). The signing-in audit accepted this only because the screen "is reached from your own name on your own sign-in, not from a shared till" (`signin-ui-audit.md` line 27–28). A shared workshop computer signed in as Jack Lewis is neither. Anyone could keep a new PIN for Jack, and so check in as the owner at a till. Signing in decision 8 judged being checked in as someone else at a till "isn’t sensitive enough" for a lock ("at worst a sale is recorded under the wrong name"), so the till side of this is a known, accepted risk; the part that is not covered is the owner pages on the same computer.
- Time-outs. Staff app: `auth-expired`, "You were signed out after a while without activity". No period is drawn (searched `signin.mjs` for "minutes" and "hours"). The sign-in spec lists the inactivity timeout as an open question (`2026-08-31-workos-auth-migration-design.md` §14, item 2). Built app: a session lasts 30 days from sign-in, fixed, not sliding (`server/auth.js` lines 17, 151, 164), and there is "no permission system yet beyond that owner flag" (lines 10–13). That is the team login the WorkOS migration replaces (`use-session.ts` line 8: "the WorkOS migration is approved but unbuilt"; the spec drops the `sessions` table), so it describes the interim, not the planned sign-in. Till: "Check-ins reset overnight" (offline spec line 82); there is no lock after wrong PINs (Signing in 8). Nothing drawn locks a workshop computer. What is drawn: an owner, or anyone with "Can change settings", can sign a device out from Signed-in devices ("Only the owner can sign out the owner’s own devices", Management oversight 3, 6 M6), but that is by hand, not a time-out.
- Weighing both: the script treats things that close by themselves on a timer as an accessibility problem (question 6), and "Don’t close things by themselves" is now a setting in Your settings (walk-through 1 L6). An unlocked shared machine is the opposite risk. Both can be met if going back to a PIN screen loses nothing.

*Why it matters:* Jack's shop has one workshop computer, and the owner is the person most likely to sign in on it "just for a minute". After that, any mechanic or Saturday worker using it sees takings and margins, can change settings and can take the owner's PIN. *Fix:*
1. **With H1 option 1, a workshop computer is never signed in as a person.** After [n] minutes with nobody touching it, it goes back to "Enter your PIN". Nothing is lost: an open job stays as it was, and the next PIN carries on from there. Owner and manager pages open there only after an Owner or Manager PIN, and "Change PIN" is not offered on a shared computer (it stays on your own sign-in, as the audit assumed). Going back to the PIN screen closes nothing and loses nothing, so it does not clash with "Don’t close things by themselves". Closes the gap, and keeps the accessibility rule. Needs H1 option 1 and a value for [n].
2. **A stated sign-out time for every email sign-in**, filling in `auth-expired`'s "after a while", shorter for Owner and Manager. Works without H1. But it is a timer that signs people out in the middle of a task, which the script counts as an accessibility problem, and signing back in means an email and password.
3. **Leave it as drawn and rely on people signing out.** Nothing to build. At Jack's shop the computer would likely sit signed in as whoever used it last.

Recommend 1. *Touches Signing in 6, 7, 8; Management oversight 3; Owner setup 8–10; walk-through 1 L6 ("Don’t close things by themselves").*

**M6 — `till-setup`, `till-rail-open`, `till-checkin` (desktop); `set-staff-invite-till-only`, `cp-ready-paid`, `job-book-in`: what a PIN check-in at a till lets you open in the rest of the shop is not said, and a till-only Saturday worker cannot reach book-in or hand-over.**
