# The go-live checklist: Jack's answers (16 Oct 2026)

Issue #140 asked for one go-live checklist for switch-over (stage 8). It is
`docs/runbooks/go-live-checklist.md`. Everything already decided went in
with its source; these are Jack's answers to what was still open, one
question each on 16 Oct. Each was Jack's choice from numbered options.

1. **Accounts software: none at switch-over.** The shop's accountant takes
   reports straight from Citrus Lime, so Wheelhouse's reports must give them
   the same; Xero and QuickBooks wait until a shop needs one.
2. **Real shop data is kept in the UK only** (chosen over UK or the EU).
3. **Records:** sales and VAT records kept 6 years for every shop; each shop
   chooses in Settings when a customer who hasn't been in for a while is
   removed, with a default the solicitor confirms (chosen over one period
   for everything, and over leaving both to the solicitor).
4. **Legal pages** (booking terms, privacy notice, cookie policy, returns,
   data agreement for shops): Claude drafts them from Jack's decisions,
   marked as drafts; Jack reads them; the solicitor checks them (chosen over
   Jack writing them, and over the solicitor writing them).
5. **The shop's own accountant; a solicitor found by the start of stage 7.**
6. **What staff must be told about what is recorded about them** is part of
   the solicitor's check (chosen over Jack checking the ICO's guidance).
7. **Jack's real export files** are kept in a private folder on his Mac,
   outside the Wheelhouse folder, with FileVault on; deleted once the two
   matching weeks have passed and Jack says so.
8. **Staff get ready** by practising on the online test copy from a short
   list, plus a one-page guide kept at the till for the first week (Jack
   added the guide).
9. **WhatsApp is needed on switch-over day** (chosen over texts first and
   WhatsApp after the trading week).
10. **Google sign-in is needed on switch-over day**, in the shop's business
    Google account.
11. **The card reader is a Stripe Reader S700**, with a phone hotspot as the
    fallback when the shop's internet is down (chosen over the S710 with its
    own mobile signal, £7 a month).
