# Journey 18, Website management — Jack's decisions (2 Oct 2026)

Journey 18 is how a shop's owner or manager sets up and changes the shop's
website inside Wheelhouse: the editor for its look and home page, its other
pages, previewing and publishing, tracking tools, its web address, connecting
online payments, and retiring the old Shopify connection. Background, not
reopened here: shops change the website through a theme system — layout,
sections, colours, fonts, images — not free-form code (Release 2 design rule
6, piece 7); it's called "Website" (Names 4) and lives under Office › Website
for owners and managers (Workshop day rooms; Names), with a "Can edit the
website" switch on a person (Owner setup 9); drawn in Soft sand by default,
nothing in the frame assuming its colours, and each shop choosing which header
link stands out as a button (App map 3, 10); the home page is a stack of
sections the shop adds, removes and reorders (Find the shop 1); shops can add
visitor-tracking tools, and only then does a cookie choice appear (Find the
shop 6); the website starts off, "Turn it on" is one press and staff see a
banner on every page while it's off (Find the shop audit M13, M14); what the
website sells, how it starts (everything or nothing), "Buying online" on or
off and the "Show on website" switches live in Settings › Front desk › Online
orders (Buy online 2, 3, 10, audit M13); online booking rules stay in
Settings › Workshop (Book a repair 11). Open outside this journey: the payment
provider is not chosen (PAY-05); custom web addresses are frozen in the
business plan (ECOM-03) — for Jack and Mark to decide. Real example data
only: North Street Cycles, Bolton, "[Second site]", Jack Lewis; other words,
photos and figures are bracketed placeholders. Own canvas:
https://claude.ai/artifact/RkyxcQZBCaVYfUa8bqixZM, desktop first, then
tablet and phone. Rules for every journey apply
(Workshop day 45, 48, 50, 53, 57, 62, 65–67; A2, A6 — as few clicks as
possible).

1. **The website is edited on a live preview** (Jack, 2 Oct: "1"). The
   website fills most of the screen as customers will see it, with a panel
   down the side listing the page's sections. Clicking a section — on the
   page or in the list — opens its settings in the panel; sections are
   dragged to reorder and added with "+ Add section"; colours and fonts sit
   under a "Theme" tab. On a phone it becomes edit, then preview. A shop
   starts from a sensible ready-made page and changes it from there. Chosen
   over a settings form with a Preview button, and ready-made themes with
   small changes.
2. **Changes wait until the shop presses Publish** (Jack, 2 Oct: "1").
   Edits save as they're made but stay private; the editor says
   "Unpublished changes", "Publish" is one press, and "Discard changes"
   throws away what hasn't gone live. A short history offers "Go back to
   this version". Scheduling a publish for later can be added without
   changing this. Chosen over every change going live at once, and
   publishing with scheduling from the start.
3. **Theme: free choice of colours, a chosen list of fonts, readability
   guarded** (Jack, 2 Oct: "1"). Any main colour and background, by picker
   or by typing the shop's colour code; when text would be hard to read,
   Wheelhouse says so and offers the nearest colour that works. About a
   dozen tested font pairs. Rounded or square corners, filled or outlined
   buttons. Fonts can be added to the list later. Chosen over fully free
   with the shop's own style code, and ready-made colour sets only.
4. **Ready-made pages to start from, built from sections, and new pages
   allowed** (Jack, 2 Oct: "1"). Contact us, Collection and returns,
   Privacy, Cookies and About us come filled in with the shop's real
   details from Settings (address, phone, opening hours) and starting
   wording for the shop to check; each page is a stack of sections in the
   same editor as the home page; "+ New page" adds more ("Bike fitting",
   "Our team"); the shop chooses which pages appear in the header or
   footer. The starting Privacy and Collection and returns wording needs a
   solicitor's check once, like the cookie wording (WEB-2); each shop stays
   responsible for its own policies. Chosen over standard pages as plain
   text only, and blank pages with a word-processor editor.
5. **Office › Website holds the website itself; selling rules stay in
   Settings › Front desk › Online orders** (Jack, 2 Oct: "1"). Office ›
   Website opens a page showing whether the site is on (Turn on / Turn
   off), its address, "Edit website" with "Unpublished changes" when there
   are any, Pages, Tracking tools and Web address. What the website sells,
   "Buying online", "Show on website" and connecting payments stay in
   Online orders (Buy online 2, 3, 10), with a link each way; payments stay
   with selling because they also serve "Pay now" for repairs (Collect and
   pay 2). Chosen over moving everything under Office › Website, and
   everything in a new Settings area.
6. **Tracking tools are picked from a list, with the tool's ID** (Jack, 2
   Oct: "1"). Office › Website › Tracking tools lists common tools (Google
   Analytics, Meta (Facebook) Pixel, Microsoft Clarity, TikTok Pixel and a
   few others); the shop switches one on and types the ID the tool gave
   it. Each tool carries its kind ("Statistics" or "Marketing"), so the
   cookie choice (Find the shop 6) asks the right questions, and nothing
   loads until the customer agrees. No pasted code. Wheelhouse's own
   cookie-free visitor numbers in Reports can be added later without
   changing this. Chosen over a box to paste any tool's code, and the list
   plus Wheelhouse's own visitor numbers now.
7. **"Use your own address" is drawn, marked as waiting on the business
   plan's freeze** (Jack, 2 Oct: "1"). Office › Website › Web address shows
   the free address ([shop-name].wheelhouseepos.com); "Use your own
   address" gives step-by-step instructions for the common registrars, a
   "Check connection" button and plain messages ("Not connected yet — this
   can take up to a day"). Custom addresses are frozen in the business plan
   (ECOM-03); whether they're built in Release 2 is for Jack and Mark, and
   the boards say so. Shops moving from Citrus Lime already have their own
   address, so keeping it is likely a condition of switching. Chosen over
   drawing only the free address, and also selling addresses through
   Wheelhouse.
8. **Online payments setup works for any provider** (Jack, 2 Oct: "1"). In
   Settings › Front desk › Online orders › Paying online, the owner presses
   "Connect [payment provider]", which opens the provider's own sign-up or
   sign-in and comes back showing "Connected"; "Make a test payment" (£1,
   refunded at once) proves it works before customers use it. Drawn too:
   connecting fails, and the provider needing more details from the shop
   later. The shop keeps its own account with the provider (sign-off CX-4);
   fees and payout wording stay placeholders until the provider is chosen
   (PAY-05). This serves the website and "Pay now" for repairs (Collect and
   pay 2). Chosen over leaving it undrawn, and drawing it for one provider.
9. **Shopify stays as a choice** (Jack, 2 Oct: "2"). Office › Website
   offers "Use Wheelhouse's website" or "Connect your Shopify shop", with
   stock and prices kept in step, for shops attached to Shopify. This
   changes the Release 2 plan, which had Wheelhouse's own website replacing
   the Shopify connection (Release 2 design, piece 7; the old app's
   connection was marked "to retire"). Chosen over retiring it, and
   keeping it only until shops have moved.
10. **With Shopify, Wheelhouse is in charge and Shopify orders come into
    Wheelhouse** (Jack, 2 Oct: "1"). Products, prices, photos and stock are
    changed in Wheelhouse and sent to Shopify automatically, "Show on
    website" deciding what appears; Shopify orders arrive in Front desk ›
    Online orders like website orders (Mark ready, Hand over) and take
    stock off at once. The shop keeps Shopify's look, editor and checkout,
    and Shopify's own payment setup for those orders. Changes made directly
    in Shopify to products, prices or stock are overwritten. Chosen over
    sending products one way with orders left in Shopify, and keeping both
    in step both ways.
11. **The first visit to Office › Website is a short three-step start, then
    the editor** (Jack, 2 Oct: "1"). (1) "Wheelhouse's website or your
    Shopify shop?"; (2) logo and main colour, with colours suggested from
    the logo; (3) "Start with every product online, or nothing" (Buy online
    3). The editor then opens on a ready-made home page filled with the
    shop's real name, address, opening hours, categories and Book a repair,
    still switched off, with "Turn it on" when ready. Pages, tracking tools,
    web address and payments are left to the Website page. Chosen over going
    straight into the editor, and a full set-up guide.
12. **UI audit: every recommendation taken** (Jack, 2 Oct: "yeah go ahead
    with them all"). From `design/user-journeys/website-ui-audit.md`:
    - The preview scrolls but can't be clicked through. Clicking a part
      selects it; choosing, adding or moving a section scrolls to it; a
      "Bigger" button hides the panel (H1).
    - **The first "Turn it on" also publishes**; after that, Turn on and Turn
      off only change who sees the last published version. Captions and
      messages say whether customers see the draft or the published version.
      Starting wording is mentioned beside "Turn it on" without blocking it
      (H2).
    - **Nothing is lost.** A discarded or replaced draft is kept in History
      for [n] days, and removing a section shows Undo (H3).
    - The hard-to-read guard stays in view with a chip by Publish, and
      Publish asks once. It checks button text, links and text on the
      background, and offers "Darken to #946F0F" or "Keep your yellow, use
      dark text" — the second adds to decision 3 (H4).
    - The Website page shows whether payments are connected, and "Buying
      online" can't be On until they are (H5).
    - Without "Can edit the website", Website isn't in the sidebar; a link to
      it says who to ask. Connecting payments follows "Can change settings";
      connecting Shopify needs both (H6).
    - Connecting Shopify shows a plain summary first, then "[n] products will
      change" and "Send [n] products". What happens to products only in
      Shopify is a rule for Jack and Mark to state (H7).
    - The editor has its own heading, main area, skip link and tab panels
      (M1). Keyboard reordering: Alt + arrow keys, greyed end arrows, the new
      position spoken, Ctrl + Z to undo (M2).
    - A save state shows ("Saved just now", "Couldn't save — trying again").
      **One person edits at a time**; a second sees "View only" or "Take
      over" (M3).
    - The Website page opens the list of unpublished changes, with Publish
      and Discard (M4). The Page menu adds Wheelhouse's shop pages
      (category, product, basket, checkout, sign in, cookie choice) to
      preview the theme, and a Tablet size (M5).
    - A visible photo description box with "This photo is only decoration"
      (M6). **Tracking tools wait for Publish** like everything else; a wrong
      ID shows no cookie choice; the shop is pointed to its Cookies and
      Privacy pages (M7).
    - Page settings for every page; Collection and returns, Privacy and
      Cookies (with a tool) can't leave the footer; new pages default to the
      footer (M8).
    - Plain web-address steps, a warning that the old website stops showing,
      named Copy buttons (M9). The test payment's refund wording waits for
      the provider, with "not tested" reminders (M10).
    - The Shopify start, the switch question and Online orders in Shopify
      mode are drawn (M11). Page previews share journey 1's header and footer
      (M12). Targets 44px (M13).
    - L1–L8: wording; Turn it on dark until the site is first on; "View
      website" after Publish; "Edit this page" for staff on the website;
      History summaries and View; an online-orders link in the editor; the
      editor names the business, as the website is one for all its shops.

    To carry into other journeys at the big-canvas step: journey 1's staff
    banner (the first Turn on publishes; "Ask [name] to turn it on" without
    the switch; "Edit this page"), and journey 2's website saying "Not
    taking online orders yet" while payments aren't connected, with the
    provider's "needs more details" warning on Today.
