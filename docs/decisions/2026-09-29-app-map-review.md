# Journey A, App map and navigation — Jack's decisions (29 Sep 2026)

Journey A is the frame around every page: the app map, the staff app shell
(sidebar by rooms, tablet rail, phone menu), till mode and the customer
website's header and menu. It is redrawn in the Soft sand look (decisions 48
and 53 of `2026-09-27-workshop-day-review.md`) on its own Design canvas,
desktop first. The rules that apply to every journey (Workshop day decisions
45, 48, 50, 53, 57, 62, 65–67) apply here too.

Already settled by Workshop day and carried over: the rooms and their pages
(Workshop room = Diary + Overview; no separate Booking requests page —
decisions 7 and 10), no date or open-until line in the sidebar (5), the
charcoal sidebar with amber only for "you are here" (48), the tablet icon
rail with labels and the phone top bar with a menu (68).

1. **Journey A is the next journey to design** (Jack, 29 Sep), chosen over
   Selling at the till, Collect the bike and pay, and Signing in — because
   every later journey sits inside its frame.
2. **One search box on every staff page** (Jack, 29 Sep): the header keeps
   a search box ("Search jobs, customers, products") on every page, so a
   name, phone number, job number or product can be found from anywhere.
   On tablet and phone it becomes a magnifying-glass button. Workshop day's
   header gains it too.
3. **The customer website is drawn in Soft sand as its default theme, with
   one extra board showing the same header in a second example theme**
   (Jack, 29 Sep), to prove the frame survives a shop's own colours. Shop
   customisation itself is a big job of its own for Release 2: journey A
   prepares for it (nothing in the frame assumes Soft sand colours) but
   does not design it.
4. **On the Till page the sidebar folds down to the icon rail** (Jack,
   29 Sep; option 3 of three drawn on the journey A canvas): the till bar
   and basket get almost the whole width, the rooms stay one tap away, and
   an Unfold button opens the full sidebar.
5. **The rail unfolds when the pointer rests on it for about 300 ms** (Jack,
   29 Sep) — no click needed; clicking Unfold still works, and on touch
   screens it is a tap. Same timing as the stacked-jobs fan-out
   (decision 59 of Workshop day). Reduce motion turns the slide off but
   keeps the unfolding.
6. **Wheelhouse-wide principle: as few clicks as possible** (Jack, 29 Sep).
   Every design should look for the step that can be dropped — hover to
   reveal, sensible defaults, one step instead of two — while keeping a
   clickable (and keyboard and touch) way to do the same thing.
7. **A folded sidebar on every page is a personal setting, off by default,
   in Settings › Accessibility** (Jack, 29 Sep): with it on, every desktop
   page shows the icon rail that unfolds on a 300 ms rest (decision 5);
   with it off (the default) pages keep the full sidebar. The Till page
   folds to the rail either way (decision 4). This adds a fourth setting to
   the Accessibility tab drawn for Workshop day (decision 57), so that
   board changes when journey A is copied in.
8. **Each person's own settings open from their name** (Jack, 29 Sep):
   clicking your name at the bottom of the sidebar (or rail, or phone menu)
   opens a "Your settings" pop-up in the middle of the screen, for every
   role. It holds the Accessibility settings now and other personal things
   later. Settings in the Office room becomes shop-wide settings only
   (Owners and Managers), so the Accessibility tab moves out of it.
9. **The "Your settings" pop-up is approved as drawn** (Jack, 29 Sep): your
   name, role and a cog as one button at the foot of the sidebar; the pop-up
   holds the four Accessibility switches, each applying at once.
10. **Each shop chooses which website header link stands out as a button**
    (Jack, 29 Sep), as part of its website theme — e.g. "Book a repair" for
    a workshop-led shop. The default theme highlights none. Jack's aim for
    the website: each shop gets as much customisation as is practical.
    Designed with the Release 2 theme work, not in journey A; the Ocean Blue
    board shows one shop's choice as an example.
11. **Where each role lands after signing in stands as drawn** (Jack,
    29 Sep): Owner and Manager → Office › Today; Staff → Front desk › Till
    (or the page they last used); Mechanic → Workshop › Diary.
