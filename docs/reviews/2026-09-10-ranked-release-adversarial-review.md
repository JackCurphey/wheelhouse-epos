## Complexity assessment and stop rules

Qualitative requirement-to-complexity score: **6/10 for the proposed release breadth**, not a measured code-quality rating. The most expensive risks are dual POS integrations and ownership rules (V3 if conflicting writes spread), a generic status/automation system (V2), a chain platform beyond capacity aggregation (V2), a new full staff/till rewrite (V2), a receipt designer despite its Later bucket (V1), and unrequested accounting/third-party settlement machinery (V2). Reduce each to the selected task and a tested contract. Removal effort and monthly maintenance cost cannot be honestly estimated from this review; establish them when packages are split, rather than inventing person-hours.

For each package, nominate a builder and a different reviewer at assignment. Jack owns product scope; technical reviewer assignment is pending. Before live enablement, record an observable success example, supported usage/load, provider cost limit where relevant, and recovery procedure. These are proposed release controls, not invented staffing commitments.

- Pause a affected live writer/worker on cross-tenant access, duplicate money/stock effects, or unexplained reconciliation drift; preserve evidence and queued work. Never auto-delete business records as a “kill”.
- Stop expanding a provider adapter if its bounded proof cannot demonstrate required capabilities/access. Return the blocked row to Jack for a scope or provider decision; do not hide the gap behind a mock.
- Review each package at its integrated demo. If it cannot identify a selected row or necessary invariant, remove the extra work from that package. Check usage/support burden after the first month of live operation before adding breadth; do not decommission a rare but necessary refund/recovery path for low usage.
- Require a simpler alternative for new infrastructure, a justification for each new runtime dependency, and at least two real consumers before introducing a generic extension mechanism. One app, Postgres, and narrow provider workers are the default.

The hard question for review: **does “all 172 before release” intentionally include two real POS integrations, online settlement, deposits and group capacity, or has a useful workshop launch become a much larger platform release through the wording of a feature inventory?** The plan preserves all 172 until Jack answers otherwise.
